How your files turn into an answer. Follow a single document with me from the moment you upload it to the moment it answers a question. Because this is where the work actually happens. When you upload files, Copilot studio stores them in Dataverse. The system then breaks each file into small pieces, turns those pieces into something called vector embeddings, and builds a semantic index over them. A vector embedding is just a way of turning meaning into numbers, so the computer can measure how close two ideas sit to each other. When someone asks a question, the agent doesn't reread the whole library. It converts the question into the same kind of numbers, finds the chunks whose meaning lands closest to the question, and hands those chunks to the model. The model then writes a clean answer from them. That's why the quality of your writing matters so much. Clear headings and plain sentences create cleaner chunks, and cleaner chunks get matched more often. Think about a long policy document with no headings and page after page of dense paragraphs. The system still cuts it into chunks, but those chunks mix several ideas together so the search gets fuzzy. Rewrite the same document with short sections and descriptive headings, and each chunk holds one clear idea. The agent now retrieves the exact paragraph a person needs, and the answer gets sharper without any change to the technology. SharePoint deserves its own warning because it gives you two doors and they behave differently. The first door copies your files into Dataverse and searches a vector index built from them. It's great for high quality semantic search, and it supports SharePoint lists. The trade off is that it consumes Dataverse storage and only refreshes every four to six hours. The second door connects straight to SharePoint and queries it live, which means no storage cost and no delay. It also supports things like sensitivity labels and filters by author or modified date. Both doors stay current on their own because a scheduled job keeps the content fresh and removes anything you deleted from the source. You just can't press a button to force a refresh, so plan around that. If your team updates a policy on Monday morning and expects the agent to know it by Monday afternoon, the copy in option will let you down while the live connector keeps up. The one setting that decides whether your agent is allowed to guess this is the setting I promised you at the start, and it has a plain name. It's called allow ungrounded responses. When it's on, your agent is free to answer from the model's general knowledge, even when no knowledge source and no tool was used. When it's off, the agent blocks any answer that didn't come from a source or a tool, and it only lets an answer through when that answer includes a citation pointing back to where it came from. For most beginners, off is the right starting point. You built this agent to answer from your content, so letting it quietly switch to guesswork defeats the whole point. There's a catch you should know about, and it's an honest one. Turning this setting off doesn't guarantee the agent never uses general knowledge because the model can still blend its own knowledge with what it retrieved. Because models sometimes forget to include a citation, a genuinely correct answer can get withheld, and the agent will act as if it found nothing.
