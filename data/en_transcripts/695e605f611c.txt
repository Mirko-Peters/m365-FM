The mistakes that bite people. Most of the pain with incremental refresh comes from five mistakes, and every one of them is avoidable once you know it's coming. The first is changing the date in the column you partitioned on incremental refresh reads a date change as a delete plus an insert. If that delete lands in your old history, the feature never picks it up, and you can end up with duplicate values or a refresh that fails on a conflict. Treat the date column as fixed. The second is expecting deleted rows to vanish. Change detection only notices soft deletes where the row is still in the source, but marked as deleted. A row that's hard deleted just stays in your model, quietly inflating your numbers. The third is republishing the file. Once a model with incremental refresh is live, publishing the same file again wipes the partitions and the history you built, and it forces a full refresh. You also can't download the model back as a file after it's published on premium. You add changes through a metadata only deployment so the history survives. The fourth is a window that's too big for the source. Some sources cap a single query at sixty four megabytes of compressed data. Log analytics and application insights are the usual culprits, and they don't always throw an error. They just quietly truncate what comes back. So smaller refresh and store periods are the fix.
