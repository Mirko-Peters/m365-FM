## Key takeaways

- Azure Policy controls which resource configurations are allowed, while RBAC controls who may act, and you need both.
- Policies are JSON if-then definitions with parameters, assigned at management group, subscription or resource group scope and inherited downward, with exemptions for edge cases.
- Start new rules in audit mode, move to deny once you are confident, and bundle related policies into initiatives for a single [compliance](https://www.m365.fm/entity/compliance/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=azure-policy-simply-explained) view.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between Azure Policy and RBAC?**

RBAC controls who can do something, while Azure Policy controls which resource configurations are allowed. RBAC might say a person can create storage accounts, and a policy says those storage accounts must have HTTPS enabled. The episode stresses that you need both, because permissions alone do not stop a misconfigured deployment.

**Should I start Azure Policy rules in audit or deny mode?**

Start in audit mode, then switch to deny once you trust the rule. Audit lets deployments go through but marks non-compliant resources in the compliance dashboard, so you see how many resources a new rule would affect. Deny blocks deployments completely and is meant for rules that must never be broken.

**What is an Azure Policy initiative?**

An initiative is a bundle of related policies assigned as one unit with a single compliance view. Instead of assigning dozens of policies separately, you assign the initiative and drill into failing policies when needed. Microsoft ships built-in initiatives for frameworks such as CIS, NIST, PCI DSS and HIPAA, and you can build custom ones.
