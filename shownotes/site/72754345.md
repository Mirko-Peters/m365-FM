## FAQ

**Why do enterprise applications become hard to maintain?**

Mostly because they were not designed to allow enhancement and modification later, says Miguel Castro. A typical warning sign is hearing that a small change would mean rewriting everything. Designing for extensibility, with parts that can be unplugged and replaced without touching the core, keeps systems maintainable and readable over time.

**When is designing for extensibility overengineering?**

For small, quick applications that are easy to maintain, building in modularity and plug-in points is overengineering because it costs extra design time. It pays off when a large system is clearly coming, or when asking where the system might change produces several answers, including cases that happen only once a year.

**Which design pattern is the basis for extensible .NET systems?**

The strategy pattern, according to Miguel, because it embraces abstraction: the backbone talks to every plug-in through the same interface. He combines it with chain of responsibility into what he calls an external events pattern, modeled on ASP.NET HTTP modules, and notes that dependency injection rests on the same idea.
