## FAQ

**What is Microsoft's internal carbon fee and how does it work?**

It is a charge Microsoft has levied on its internal business divisions since 2012 per metric ton of emissions tied to their activity. It covers scopes 1, 2 and 3, has been revised and increased over time, and the proceeds are reinvested into efficiency, renewable energy and supplier decarbonization, so carbon behaves like a budget line.

**Why did Microsoft's emissions rise despite its carbon negative goal?**

Emissions rose because the platform grew, mainly through the infrastructure build-out for AI and data centers. AI adds denser, power-hungry hardware and more supply chain emissions, so operational and scope 3 emissions both increase. The episode frames this as an architectural consequence of scale rather than hypocrisy, which makes carbon removals a necessary part of the target.

**How can an organization run a carbon review on a single cloud workload?**

Pick one production workload, assign one accountable owner and draw a defensible boundary. Collect monthly cost, uptime and performance targets, deployment region and runtime shape, then add a consistent emissions estimate. Ask whether you would still deploy it this way if carbon were priced like cost, make one change this week and write a default policy for new workloads.
