## Key takeaways

- Enterprise Azure migrations fail systemically, not technically: treat them as an operating model change and build the [landing zone](https://www.m365.fm/entity/azure-landing-zone/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=enterprise-azure-migration-strategy-how-to-move-legacy-systems-to-microsoft-cloud-without-breaking-compliance-governance-or-the-business) contract for identity, policy, network and logging before the first workload moves.
- Use an environment-first management group hierarchy, shared services subscriptions and hub-and-spoke networking, and treat prioritization as portfolio management across rehost, replatform, refactor and retire.
- Run a migration factory with repeatable validation gates, earn modernization through platform maturity, and turn the CCOE into a platform product team that offers paved roads instead of approvals.

## FAQ

**Why do enterprise Azure migrations fail even when the cutover succeeds?**

They fail systemically, not technically, because leadership treats migration as an IT project instead of an operating model change. Workloads move before identity, policy, logging and approval flows are enforced, so teams improvise access grants, shared accounts and spreadsheet evidence. Governance debt then builds faster than delivery can repay it, and audits expose the gaps.

**Should management groups in Azure follow the org chart?**

No, the episode recommends an environment-first hierarchy of prod, non-prod and sandbox, then splits by exposure or regulation. Org charts change faster than policy, so mirroring them turns reorganizations into security changes. Management groups are policy inheritance surfaces, while subscriptions act as blast radius boundaries that contain failures, cost blowouts and mistakes.

**Which workloads should move to Azure first?**

Start with a moderately critical, standard-stack workload such as a customer-facing web app, and replatform it to test the landing zone contract with tolerable consequences. Core batch systems that cannot tolerate change are rehosted with deterministic rollback and validation gates. Low-value shadow IT reporting is retired, because the cheapest modernization is deletion.
