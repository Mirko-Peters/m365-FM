## FAQ

**Why does Azure governance fail even when policies exist?**

It usually fails through drift, not missing controls. Reasonable, urgent exceptions get approved, are rarely tracked or removed, and slowly replace the baseline. The episode argues every exception needs an owner, a reason, an expiry date and revalidation, otherwise it becomes a permanent fork in system behavior that audits and incidents later expose.

**When should you use deny, audit, modify or deploy-if-not-exists in Azure Policy?**

Use deny for non-negotiable boundaries, audit only for learning and rollout, modify for hygiene such as tags and standard settings, and deploy-if-not-exists for platform requirements like diagnostics. The episode warns that deploying policies without remediating existing resources creates a split-brain tenant where new resources comply and older ones do not.

**How does Privileged Identity Management improve Azure RBAC?**

PIM separates eligibility from activation, so privileged access becomes a time-bound, audited event instead of a standing right. The episode recommends roles assigned to groups rather than users, expiring activations, real justifications, MFA and approvals for boundary-changing roles such as Owner and User Access Administrator, with break-glass accounts kept loud and reviewed.
