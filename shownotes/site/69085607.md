Many Microsoft 365 governance programs spend years turning red into amber without ever reaching green. This episode is for IT, security and compliance leads who rely on Purview scores, readiness reviews and Power BI dashboards to prove control. It follows five cycles of reviews, budgets, audits and true-ups to show how metrics create false control, and what simpler, enforceable governance looks like.

## Key takeaways

- Maturity scores, readiness indices and heat maps measure motion and documentation, not reduced exposure, so they can keep governance programs running without changing outcomes.
- Exceptions in [Conditional Access](https://www.m365.fm/entity/conditional-access/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=microsoft-teams-governance-illusion-control), Teams policies and PIM, report-only policies and audit-only DLP quietly become the real control model unless they carry an end date and a review loop.
- Simple, human-readable rules backed by small automations - expiring stale guests, claiming ownerless groups, reviewing policy changes - can deliver more control than ever-growing licensing and workshops.

## FAQ

**Why does a Microsoft Purview Compliance Manager score not prove governance?**

A Compliance Manager score measures documented improvement actions, not reduced exposure. In the episode the score hovers at 63 while DLP policies stay in audit-only mode, guests keep receiving tokens and ownerless groups remain. The amber score justifies more budget and workshops, because it never reaches a point where stopping the program looks responsible.

**How do Conditional Access and Teams policy exceptions undermine Microsoft 365 governance?**

Exceptions undermine governance when they have no end date or review. The episode shows MFA exclusions for executives, service accounts and trusted IP ranges, PIM break-glass carve-outs and a Teams guest bypass created for one board meeting. Each was logged with a justification, but none was ever removed, so the exceptions became the real control model.

**What is a simpler alternative to governance maturity programs in Microsoft 365?**

A short list of readable rules backed by small automations is the alternative presented. Examples include a scheduled job that posts ownerless groups until someone claims them, revoking guests inactive for 90 days, and a weekly Conditional Access change report requiring sign-off. Rules like no anonymous links and no new policy without deleting an old one replace scores.
