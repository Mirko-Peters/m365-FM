## Key takeaways

- Every label must map to a clearly stated business risk, starting with a small core set such as internal only, personal data, confidential and public.
- Over-engineered label hierarchies and poorly prepared users create workarounds, so simplicity and clear communication drive adoption.
- Pilot with real business users under real deadlines and reinforce training through microlearning, contextual nudges and manager follow-up.

## FAQ

**Why do Microsoft Information Protection rollouts fail?**

Most rollouts fail because of people and planning, not technology. The episode names five trip wires: labels with no clear purpose, technical over-engineering, user resistance, pilots run only by IT and training that fades. Missing any one of them lets adoption slip, even when the platform is configured perfectly in the admin console.

**How many sensitivity labels should you start with?**

Start with a handful of core labels tied to your biggest risks, such as internal only, personal data, confidential and possibly public. The episode warns that twenty labels or a twelve-level hierarchy confuse users. Expand only after usage shows employees apply the labels consistently, and tie every new label to a business requirement.

**How do you know if a sensitivity label pilot is successful?**

A pilot succeeds when real business users label correctly under real deadlines. The episode suggests checking whether at least eighty percent apply the correct label during live tasks, whether support tickets per hundred users are stable or falling, and whether reporting, collaboration and client emails complete without unplanned blocks.
