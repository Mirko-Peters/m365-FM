## FAQ

**Is a Microsoft Fabric capacity a security or cost boundary?**

No, a Fabric capacity is a shared compute pool, not a project, tenant or safety boundary. When one team runs a heavy refresh, other workspaces on the same capacity inherit contention, throttling and smoothing effects. Workspace names such as dev or prod do not isolate execution, so governance has to be stronger than the workspace abstraction.

**Why does Microsoft Fabric spend rise when usage looks stable?**

Fabric spend drifts because the estate accumulates duplication, background operations and contention even when reports and users stay the same. Mirroring, lakehouse copies, extra semantic models and overlapping refreshes all add load to the shared pool. Teams then scale capacity up to stabilize production, and the larger capacity quietly becomes the permanent baseline.

**How should chargeback work in Microsoft Fabric?**

Chargeback should allocate cost to an owner with the power to delete, never to a container such as a workspace. Workspace-based allocation is easy to game, because teams can split, move or rename workloads. A workload, data product or domain owner with enforcement authority can retire or consolidate assets, while unowned workloads should be quarantined.
