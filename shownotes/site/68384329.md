## Key takeaways

- In Microsoft Fabric compute, not storage, drives cost, so every duplicated ingestion or refresh directly multiplies the bill.
- Use bronze Dataflows Gen2 to land each external source once, silver dataflows to centralize business logic and quality, and a gold layer with Direct Lake to serve consumers without extra refreshes.
- Never mix ingestion and transformation in the same dataflow, and expose gold outputs centrally instead of copying tables into every department's [workspace](https://www.m365.fm/entity/fabric-workspace/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=stop-wasting-money-the-3-architectures-for-fabric-data-flows-gen-2).

## FAQ

**Why do Dataflows Gen2 make Microsoft Fabric so expensive?**

Usually because teams use them like Power BI imports. In Fabric, compute is what you pay for, and every refresh spins up compute. When each department's workspace ingests the same source system separately and then refreshes dependent datasets again, the same work runs several times, so costs multiply while storage stays cheap.

**What is the difference between bronze, silver and gold dataflows in Fabric?**

Bronze lands each external source once as Delta tables with only light normalization. Silver references those tables to apply business logic, lookups and data quality rules in one central place. Gold serves the curated output through Direct Lake semantic models or shared tables, so consumers read data without triggering extra refreshes or copies.

**Do small teams need all three Fabric dataflow architectures?**

No. For a small team the episode suggests one bronze dataflow for ingestion and a thin silver layer, served directly through Direct Lake without a separate gold workspace. Enterprises with many domains need all three layers, while mixed projects can combine bronze and silver and add a minimal gold workspace for executive reporting.
