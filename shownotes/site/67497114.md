## FAQ

**When should I use Microsoft To Do instead of Planner?**

Use Microsoft To Do for your own daily reminders and Planner when several people share a deliverable. To Do works as a personal scratch pad for items like calling a vendor or preparing slides. Planner adds boards, buckets and assignments so everyone sees who owns what and how the work is progressing.

**What is the difference between Microsoft Lists and Planner for project tracking?**

Planner is built for team visibility on tasks, while Microsoft Lists handles structured data. If your work depends on multiple stages, metadata or fields you need to sort and filter, such as asset tracking or equipment requests, Lists gives you columns, workflows and controlled views. Pushing that data into Planner leaves a cluttered, overloaded board.

**How can Power Automate keep To Do, Planner, Lists and Outlook in sync?**

Power Automate connects the task tools through ready-made templates, so no coding is needed. Examples from the episode include creating a To Do task from a flagged Outlook email and creating a Planner task from a new list item. The real skill is spotting where information gets retyped, copied or lost, then automating that step.
