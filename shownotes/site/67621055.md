## FAQ

**Why do so many Microsoft 365 Copilot rollouts fail to show ROI?**

Most rollouts fail because organizations measure access instead of outcomes. They count activated licenses and logins, skip baselines of how long workflows took before Copilot, and never connect usage to time saved or output produced. Without that comparison, leaders cannot tell whether Copilot changed anything, so rollout success turns into guesswork based on surface numbers.

**Which metrics show whether Copilot adoption is actually working?**

Behavioral metrics tell you more than license counts. The episode points to usage surface area across real workflows, frequency of task-specific queries, consistency of use across departments and the variety of tasks Copilot supports. Combined with a pre-rollout baseline, these signals show whether Copilot is becoming part of daily work or staying a novelty.

**How do you turn Copilot usage data into a feedback loop?**

You turn usage data into a feedback loop by acting on it instead of only reporting it. If most prompts are email drafting, targeted training can introduce advanced scenarios such as summarizing meeting notes or drafting proposals. Uneven adoption between departments becomes a signal to share best practices from high-performing teams with those that lag behind.
