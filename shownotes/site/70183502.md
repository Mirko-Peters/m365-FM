## FAQ

**What is a control plane in Microsoft 365 governance?**

A control plane is the layer that decides what may exist, who may create it, which rules must hold at creation and what happens when they stop holding. The episode builds it from an Entra identity policy engine, Microsoft Graph-based provisioning and DLP enforcement, so governance happens by default rather than through human review.

**Why don't approval boards scale for Power Platform governance?**

Human review is the slowest component, so it becomes the throughput limit for the whole tenant. Makers who hit the queue route around it through the default environment, shared connections or personal connectors. Approved exceptions become precedent, and the board ends up negotiating entropy instead of enforcing policy. The episode argues the only scalable review board is code.

**How can you measure whether a governance control plane is working?**

Track governance-related support tickets: ownership transfers, access requests, connector approvals, DLP blocks, environment drift and broken connections. Tag tickets for 30 to 60 days as a baseline, roll out the control plane gates, then measure volume, time to resolution, reopen rate and escalations to admins. In the episode's first case study these tickets dropped 41 percent.
