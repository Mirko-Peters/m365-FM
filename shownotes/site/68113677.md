## Key takeaways

- A single compromised user account can escalate to domain admin through lateral movement, cached credentials and pass-the-hash if Active Directory is left on defaults.
- Reducing domain admins, enforcing a tiered least-privilege model and restricting service accounts removes the most common escalation paths.
- Certificate templates and hybrid sync connectors need regular audits, logging and consistent [MFA](https://www.m365.fm/entity/mfa/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=active-directory-the-crown-jewel-hackers-hunt) and Conditional Access, or they become hidden paths into both on-premises and cloud.

## FAQ

**How can one compromised user account lead to domain admin?**

Through lateral movement on a default Active Directory. Attackers harvest cached credentials, use pass-the-hash and look for admin tokens left on workstations. Shared local admin passwords and privileged accounts logging into ordinary machines let them climb quickly. Because each hop is a valid connection, monitoring tuned for brute force or failed logins often misses it.

**Why are AD certificate templates a security risk?**

Because misconfigured templates let ordinary domain users request certificates that grant elevated rights. The encryption itself is solid; the problem is who may enroll and what the template allows. A signed certificate looks like a clean login and can bypass MFA if not designed carefully. Audit templates, strip broad enrollment rights and log every request.

**How do I secure hybrid identity between AD and the cloud?**

Treat sync connectors with the same rigor as domain controllers. Audit your Microsoft Entra Connect (formerly Azure AD Connect) configuration, minimize sync rights, harden federation against token replay and enforce MFA and Conditional Access consistently on-premises and in the cloud. Correlate sign-ins, certificate issuance and connector activity, treating cloud and on-premises logs as one system.
