## FAQ

**Why do successful Power Platform pilots fail when rolled out across the enterprise?**

Pilots fail at scale because they prove usefulness, not scalability. A pilot runs on narrow scope, a clear owner, short decision paths and shared context that people fill in informally. When more teams and regions adopt it, that hidden human support disappears, and missing ownership, lifecycle, support and decision rights surface all at once.

**What is the difference between replicating a solution and scaling it?**

Replication copies outputs such as apps, flows, site templates or policy packs, while scaling reproduces the operating conditions that made the original trustworthy. Teams can share the same platform and templates yet handle ownership, access reviews and exceptions differently. That creates drift behind one interface, which Mirko Peters calls inconsistency hidden by common tooling.

**What should leaders measure instead of adoption when scaling Microsoft 365 and Power Platform?**

Leaders should measure system health rather than usage alone. The episode suggests decision velocity, duplication load, ownership clarity, dependency load and the ratio of environments to delivered business value. Leading indicators include more private channels, duplicate tools, manual reconciliation between reports and exceptions handled outside the normal path, which show stress before failures become visible.
