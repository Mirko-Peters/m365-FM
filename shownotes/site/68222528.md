## Key takeaways

- Dataverse combines security role privileges additively, so a single organization-level or parent-child scope given to a guest overrides every narrower restriction.
- Isolate external users in their own child business unit, build guest roles from scratch with user-level scope, use teams and access teams for record sharing and field-level security for sensitive columns.
- Keep the model safe with regular reviews in the [Power Platform admin center](https://www.m365.fm/entity/power-platform-admin-center/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=dataverse-security-external-access), Purview DLP policies, Dataverse auditing, Entra ID group-based cleanup and role checks before every deployment.

## FAQ

**Why can guest users see too much data in Dataverse?**

Usually because a security role grants them parent-child or organization-level read. Dataverse combines role privileges additively, so the broadest scope wins over any narrower role. A guest role cloned from an internal salesperson role, for example, can expose every contact under the parent business unit to a vendor portal user.

**How should I set up business units for external users in Dataverse?**

Put external users in their own isolated child business unit, ideally at the lowest level of the hierarchy. Because parent-child scope flows downward, internal users never inherit visibility from the guest unit, while you can still share specific records with guests. Combine this with guest roles built from scratch using user-level scope.

**How do I test a Dataverse security role for guests safely?**

Test with a dummy guest account, never with an admin or a cloned admin identity. Create the test user in the external business unit, assign the proposed role and check which records it can see. If the dummy guest sees more than its own record, the role is scoped too broadly and needs fixing.
