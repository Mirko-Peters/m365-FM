## Key takeaways

- No-code speed comes from skipping checkpoints such as reviews, staged testing and release gates, so you need to know which guardrails you are trading away for agility.
- Low-code platforms give you inherited guardrails, DLP-style policies and vendor compliance attestations, while pro-code gives full control but makes you design, maintain and prove every control yourself.
- Choose the model by risk ownership: the sensitivity of the data, who answers when something breaks, and whether you need integrations beyond your standard stack.

## FAQ

**Is low-code more secure than pro-code development?**

Neither is a clear winner. Low-code platforms give you guardrails you did not design, such as DLP-style connector policies, applied consistently but hard to customize. Pro-code gives full control over every security layer, but nothing is enforced automatically, so your team has to design, test and maintain each safeguard as the system evolves.

**How does compliance differ between low-code apps and custom-coded apps?**

Low-code apps can lean on the vendor's published attestations for many baseline requirements, while custom builds must produce their own evidence. That can mean archived logs, monitoring alerts and key-rotation records. Inherited compliance only covers standard use, so anything that handles data in non-standard ways or connects to third-party systems still needs your own controls.

**How do I decide between low-code and pro-code for a new app?**

Base the choice on risk ownership rather than speed or features. Ask what level of data the app handles, who answers when something breaks, and whether you need integrations beyond your standard stack. Also check your most critical non-standard systems for maintained connectors or published APIs before committing to either approach.
