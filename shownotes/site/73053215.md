## Key takeaways

- Choosing a monorepo or multi-repo is really choosing where coordination happens: visibly in code review and CI, or invisibly in APIs, contracts and meetings.
- Fragmentation comes from organizational debt such as unclear ownership, slow decision paths and inconsistent processes, and no repository strategy fixes it on its own.
- Match your repository model to how your teams are actually coupled, measure coordination overhead and flow efficiency, and commit to the infrastructure or governance your choice requires.

## FAQ

**Is a monorepo better than a multi-repo?**

Neither is objectively better; both scale technically. A monorepo makes coordination visible in code review and CI, while a multi-repo moves it into APIs, contracts, versioning and meetings. The episode advises choosing the model that matches how tightly your teams are actually coupled, or a hybrid when only core services must move together.

**Why do monorepos become slow as organizations grow?**

Monorepos slow down mainly because of organizational debt, not Git limits. As more teams share the code, reviews need more sign-offs, dependency upgrades stall and builds grow. Sparse checkout, partial clone, selective builds and caching help, but they only treat symptoms when ownership and decision rights remain unclear.

**How can you measure fragmentation in an engineering organization?**

Measure coordination overhead and flow efficiency instead of counting repositories or build times. Track over a representative two-week period how much time is spent building versus waiting for reviews, approvals or other teams. Then follow a real cross-team change to see where it got stuck, which shows where organizational debt sits.
