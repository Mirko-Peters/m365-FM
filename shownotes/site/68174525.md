## Key takeaways

- Copilot inherits Microsoft 365 governance such as tenant boundaries, Purview and [audit logs](https://www.m365.fm/entity/audit-logs/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=copilots-compliant-by-design-claim-exposed), while standalone ChatGPT leaves deployers to build those safeguards themselves.

## FAQ

**Does the EU AI Act apply to companies that only use Copilot or ChatGPT?**

Yes. The Act spreads obligations across the AI supply chain, so organizations deploying Copilot or ChatGPT share responsibility with the vendor. The episode stresses that auditors will ask for the deployer's own records, risk assessments and logs, not just evidence that Microsoft or OpenAI offer compliance features.

**Is Microsoft 365 Copilot classified as high risk under the EU AI Act?**

Not by default; the classification depends on how you use it. Summarizing meeting notes sits at limited or minimal risk, but feeding Copilot into hiring, credit scoring or compliance reporting can push the same tool up to high risk, with documentation, human oversight, logging and staff training obligations attached.

**Are Copilot prompts used to train Microsoft's foundation models?**

No, according to Microsoft's documentation cited in the episode. Prompts, responses and data accessed through Microsoft Graph are not used to train the foundation models, and Copilot runs on Azure OpenAI inside the Microsoft 365 service boundary. The episode notes that the EU Data Boundary can still flex to other regions during peak demand.
