## FAQ

**What is identity debt in Microsoft Entra ID?**

Identity debt is standing privilege combined with ungoverned exceptions and unowned identities. Examples include permanent tenant-wide roles, Conditional Access exclusions without owner or expiry, legacy protocol carve-outs, service principals with no owner and guests without sponsors. The episode argues it accumulates by default because temporary exceptions become permanent while the policy engine keeps enforcing whatever remains.

**Which Conditional Access policies belong in a minimal baseline?**

Three policies: block legacy authentication for all users with no exclusions, require MFA for all users and cloud apps, and require phishing-resistant authentication strengths for privileged roles and critical apps. Only two cloud-only break-glass accounts should be excluded from MFA, each with an owner and expiry, plus an alert whenever a sign-in shows policy not applied.

**How should service principals and workload identities be secured?**

By fixing scope and lifecycle, because workload identities never see MFA prompts. The episode recommends managed identities inside Azure, federated credentials for external pipelines such as GitHub Actions, certificates instead of client secrets where app registrations remain, minimal Graph permissions, resource-level role assignments and an accountable owner for every enterprise app.
