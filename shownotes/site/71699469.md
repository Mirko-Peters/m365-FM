## Key takeaways

- Global edge services and DNS are dependencies that need regional bypass paths, not invisible utilities.
- Recovery plans that depend on redeploying through the control plane fail when everyone hits the same management APIs and capacity runs out, so pre-provision warm standbys.
- Resilience comes from a deliberate state strategy, automated failover governance and regular chaos testing.

## FAQ

**Why isn't deploying to multiple Azure regions enough for resilience?**

Because the regions often share hidden dependencies. A global edge service such as Azure Front Door, DNS resolution or the Azure Resource Manager control plane can fail across regions, and replicated data can lag behind. Without bypass paths, reserved capacity and a state strategy, multi-region becomes a distributed single point of failure.

**Why does a redeploy-on-demand disaster recovery plan fail during a regional outage?**

Because both the control plane and capacity become scarce. When a region goes down, thousands of organizations hit the same management APIs at once, causing timeouts and 503 errors, and popular VM sizes in neighboring regions sell out quickly. The episode recommends warm standbys so recovery only needs a routing change.

**How should failover decisions be governed during a cloud outage?**

Through automated circuit breakers driven by telemetry instead of war-room meetings. Thresholds such as sustained backbone latency should trigger the traffic shift automatically. The platform team supplies approved patterns, while the application team owns the runbook and has the predefined right to fail over, even before the provider declares a disaster.
