## FAQ

**Why do Azure applications fail under heavy load when the dashboard looks healthy?**

Most failures come from design decisions, not from the Azure platform itself. Autoscale thresholds set too loosely trigger scaling too late, hidden dependencies slow database calls, and load tests with a handful of users never reveal what happens when thousands arrive on Monday morning. The dashboard catches up only after customers have already seen errors.

**What are the five pillars of a resilient Azure architecture?**

The five pillars are availability, redundancy, elasticity, observability and security. Availability keeps services reachable across zones or regions, redundancy removes single points of failure, elasticity scales with demand, observability exposes problems before users notice, and security prevents attacks or identity misconfigurations from taking systems offline. None of them works well alone.

**Are backups and disaster recovery enough to protect an Azure workload?**

No, backups and disaster recovery restore infrastructure after a failure but do not stop the damage while it happens. Customers still experience failed checkouts and stalled workflows during the gap. Mirroring to another region also cannot fix a misconfigured scaling policy, so resilience has to be designed into the architecture from day one.
