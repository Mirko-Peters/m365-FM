## FAQ

**Why are written governance policies not enough in Microsoft 365?**

Written policies only state intent; they depend on a busy person noticing, remembering and choosing correctly in the moment. Mirko Peters calls user memory the single point of failure. Controls such as default labels, DLP and clean permissions act automatically when an action happens, so they hold even when people are rushed, tired or under deadline pressure.

**What are digital desire paths in Microsoft 365?**

Digital desire paths are the repeated workarounds people use when the official route is too slow, such as exporting to Excel, sharing broad links or moving decisions into private chats. The episode treats them as design feedback, not misconduct, and suggests triaging each path: block dangerous ones, redirect valid needs and pave useful routes inside the governed environment.

**How should DLP balance blocking and nudging users?**

DLP should match its response to the actual risk: hard blocks for high-confidence attempts to move sensitive data out, and nudges such as policy tips for medium-risk actions. The episode argues that many hard blocks in normal workflows signal that the safe path arrives too late, while effective nudging shapes behavior earlier with less friction.
