## Key takeaways

- Intelligent versioning with co-authoring and autosave preserves fewer near-term versions than edits, so retention keeps versions, not every edit.
- Short-lived content such as meeting recordings, temp exports and [OneDrive](https://www.m365.fm/entity/onedrive/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=microsoft-365-compliance-drift-explained) spillover is often deleted before retention labels arrive, so governance succeeds on a reduced set.
- Track three variance signals (creation ratio, survival hit rate, discovery coverage ratio) and treat flat dashboards in a growing tenant as incidents until explained.

## FAQ

**Why does SharePoint version history show fewer versions than actual edits?**

Co-authoring and autosave consolidate rapid edits into fewer versions at creation, before any trimming. In the episode's tests, single-author sessions produced about two or three file-modified events per version and co-authoring five or six. Saves within a few seconds coalesce, so retention keeps the versions that exist, not every edit.

**Why do meeting recordings and temporary files often never get a retention label?**

They are deleted before governance clocks catch up. Retention policy distribution can take up to seven days and auto-apply labels run on background cycles, while recordings and temp exports were often removed within one to three days. The fix is governing them at birth in labeled locations or enforcing a minimum holding period.

**Why do eDiscovery search results stay flat while the tenant keeps growing?**

Because eDiscovery searches what still exists, not what was created. Consolidated versions and early cleanup shrink the corpus upstream, so identical KQL returns steady counts and execution times. The episode recommends tracking a discovery coverage ratio, discoverable items against uploads from the audit log, and fixing birth locations before widening queries.
