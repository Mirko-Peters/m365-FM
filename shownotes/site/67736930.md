## FAQ

**When should I move work from Power Automate to an Azure Function?**

Move work to an Azure Function when a flow spends most of its time looping over data or wrestling with complex API calls. Typical cases are heavy compute, such as parsing a twenty thousand line CSV, and calling internal APIs without an available connector. The flow simply calls the function and picks up cleaned results.

**What are the warning signs that a Power Platform solution is hitting its limits?**

The main warning signs are growing run durations, repeated retries or throttling errors, and patchwork nested flows built to mimic logic. One sign alone may not be urgent, but two or three together suggest the solution is out of room. Checking flow run histories for retries, delays and unexplained failures reveals these signals early.

**Should I use AI Builder or Azure AI services in my Power Apps and flows?**

Start with AI Builder for quick wins such as extracting fields from forms or building a simple prediction model. Move to Azure AI services when you need fine-grained control, multi-language support, specialized APIs or better handling of unstructured documents like scanned invoices and receipts. The choice depends on the problem's scope, not on one tool being better.
