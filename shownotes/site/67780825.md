## FAQ

**What is the difference between a SharePoint content map and a content assessment?**

A content map catalogs what you have and where it sits, while an assessment asks whether files still matter, who touches them and how they fit into business workflows. A map shows where files should live, not how accessible they really are, so a tidy structure can still hide clutter and findability problems.

**How can I use search logs to find findability gaps in Microsoft 365?**

Export your top search queries for a 30-day window and look for queries with low result clicks or many repeated searches. Then map those queries to the files or libraries that should satisfy them. Where results miss expectations, you have found a gap, often caused by user language not matching titles or metadata.

**What should a content assessment report for leadership include?**

It should include a one-page executive summary, the top pain points with their business impact, quick wins, medium-term projects with next steps, long-term governance commitments and named owners with KPIs. Leaders need a clear story about the current situation, the cost of leaving it as is and the opportunity, not a pile of dashboards.
