## Key takeaways

- Copilot adoption succeeds when it starts from department pain points and comes with real training, not when AI is rolled out for its own sake.

## FAQ

**How should a company start a Microsoft 365 Copilot rollout?**

Start with a clear AI plan built on department pain points instead of rolling out AI for its own sake. Chris Hinch suggests a small pilot in one department such as HR, finance or legal to prove a concrete use case, combined with proper training so users actually save time rather than waste it.

**Does Microsoft 365 Copilot use company data to train models?**

No. According to Chris Hinch, Microsoft does not use customer data from Copilot for training, and agreements with sub-processors prevent them from using prompts for training as well. Copilot also respects the permissions of the person using it, so it only reaches content that user can already access.

**Can Copilot expose oversharing and poor permissions?**

Yes. Because Copilot respects user permissions, existing oversharing becomes easier to surface. Chris points to SharePoint Advanced Management, included with Copilot, to restrict sites from Copilot search, plus Purview labels, Entra group controls and Defender blocking of risky AI apps, and recommends checking Microsoft's baseline security mode settings.
