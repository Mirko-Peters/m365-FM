Teams meetings are often treated as video calls, while the real work happens in other tabs and after the call. This episode shows Teams developers and solution architects how custom meeting apps, side panels and Microsoft Graph lifecycle events combine into a meeting that brings live data in and hands follow-up work off automatically.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – The core building blocks of Teams meeting extensibility
  • 04:22 – Custom apps embedded with zero disruption
  • 07:46 – Side panels: real-time context without switching tabs
  • 11:30 – Graph meeting lifecycle events as the automated conductor
  • 15:05 – Wiring all three into one system

Key takeaways

  • Teams meeting extensibility rests on three interoperable pieces: custom meeting apps, in-meeting side panels and Microsoft Graph meeting lifecycle events.
  • Meeting apps feel native when the manifest, context-aware deep links and single sign-on are configured so participants never leave the meeting or face extra prompts.
  • Subscribing to started, ended and participant events through Graph webhooks lets the meeting itself trigger data syncs, side panel refreshes and follow-up tasks.

Worth quoting

“Each of these has its own development model, permissions, and deployment considerations, but none depend entirely on the others to work. They're interoperable, not interdependent.” – Mirko Peters (01:36)

“That invisibility is actually the goal, because it means the tool is supporting the meeting, not competing with it for attention.” – Mirko Peters (07:29)
