Training users to write better prompts will not fix Microsoft 365 Copilot if your tenant has no governed place where intent and truth persist. This episode is for architects, IT leaders and governance teams who want reliable Copilot answers, showing how persistent context, Copilot Notebooks, Entra and Purview turn plausible output into defensible decisions.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Copilot is not a chatbot
  • 05:19 – What persistent context is and is not
  • 09:18 – Failure modes: hallucinated policy, context sprawl and context rot
  • 26:34 – Why Copilot Notebooks exist and context engineering
  • 35:12 – Broken RAG at scale and Entra as the real control plane
  • 43:49 – Purview and the OneNote, Pages, Notebook triad
  • 49:48 – The context design checklist
  • 61:46 – Receipts, automation and KPIs that matter

Key takeaways

  • Copilot failures are usually selection and context architecture problems, not prompting problems, because the prompt only steers which tenant fragments get retrieved.
  • Persistent context means reusable intent plus curated, authoritative sources with owners, lifecycle and review cadence, and Copilot Notebooks are the container that binds them for a defined domain.
  • Entra permissions and Purview labels, retention and DLP define the real context boundary, so reliable Copilot outcomes require governed identity, traceable outputs and KPIs for quality, cost and control.

Worth quoting

“The prompt doesn't create truth. The prompt only steers, which fragments of your tenant co-pilot will consider while it manufactures a response.” – Mirko Peters (02:04)
