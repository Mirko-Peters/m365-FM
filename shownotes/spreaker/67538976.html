Copilot feels vague and unreliable in many tenants, but the AI model is rarely the problem. This episode is for Microsoft 365 admins and Copilot owners who want sharper answers, showing how cleaning SharePoint libraries, fixing permissions, enforcing metadata and connecting Power Automate turns Copilot into a precision tool.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why Copilot feels underwhelming: SharePoint clutter
  • 04:19 – Blind spots from broken permissions
  • 08:02 – Power Automate as Copilot's missing engine
  • 12:58 – Inconsistent metadata and scattered conversations
  • 17:17 – Preparing the whole Microsoft 365 ecosystem
  • 21:36 – The weekly audit test

Key takeaways

  • Cluttered SharePoint libraries with duplicate 'final' files and missing metadata force Copilot to guess, producing vague answers.
  • Broken or stale permissions hide large parts of the data estate from Copilot, so role-based access and regular access reviews are prerequisites for complete answers.
  • Copilot delivers real value when governed SharePoint, consistent permissions, mandatory metadata and Power Automate flows work together as one prepared system.

Worth quoting

“Copilot is only as smart as the access it's given. You could have the cleanest, most well labeled data set in the world, but if the AI can't reach half of it, you'll never see its full potential.” – Mirko Peters (07:24)

“Good metadata works like a road sign. It points Copilot directly to what's relevant. Without it, the system has nothing to distinguish between two files with near identical names.” – Mirko Peters (02:50)
