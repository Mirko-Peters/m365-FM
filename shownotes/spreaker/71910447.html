Microservices promised speed, but many .NET teams now spend more time on contracts, tracing and coordination than on features. This episode explains the hidden costs of distributed architectures and gives architects and engineering leads a practical model for deciding what should be a service and what should stay a module.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Microservices sold as autonomy
  • 03:01 – The hidden tax of distributed complexity
  • 06:39 – API contracts as digital red tape
  • 10:09 – Distributed transactions in modern .NET
  • 13:29 – The market correction
  • 17:00 – A decision model for .NET teams

Key takeaways

  • Microservices add network, observability, mesh and coordination costs that often outweigh their benefits for smaller teams.
  • Rigid API contracts and manual governance turn into digital red tape; move checks into CI and pull requests instead.
  • Default to a modular monolith with clear internal boundaries and separate services only when a concrete problem requires it today.

Worth quoting

“Microservices pay off later, not early. For teams under roughly 20 to 30 engineers, the coordination cost often beats the scaling benefit. You don't get independence. You get dependency with more steps.” – Mirko Peters (01:24)

“Strong governance can help velocity, but only when it behaves like infrastructure and not committee process.” – Mirko Peters (09:55)
