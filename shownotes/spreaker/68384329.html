Many Microsoft Fabric projects burn compute because teams still use Dataflows Gen2 like Power BI's old import sidecar. This episode is for data engineers and Power BI teams who want lower Fabric bills and cleaner lineage. It explains the three architectures - bronze staging, silver transformation and gold serving - and when to use each.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why Fabric dataflows waste money
  • 01:28 – The core misunderstanding: compute is the meter
  • 04:08 – Architecture 1: bronze staging for shared ingestion
  • 08:21 – Architecture 2: silver transformation and quality
  • 13:34 – Architecture 3: gold serving for consumption
  • 19:04 – Choosing the right architecture
  • 22:51 – Closing argument

Key takeaways

  • In Microsoft Fabric compute, not storage, drives cost, so every duplicated ingestion or refresh directly multiplies the bill.
  • Use bronze Dataflows Gen2 to land each external source once, silver dataflows to centralize business logic and quality, and a gold layer with Direct Lake to serve consumers without extra refreshes.
  • Never mix ingestion and transformation in the same dataflow, and expose gold outputs centrally instead of copying tables into every department's workspace.

Worth quoting

“Bronze is about reliable landings. Everything landing here should be traceable back to an external source historically intact and refreshable, independently, think raw but usable, not pretty and modeled.” – Mirko Peters (05:29)
