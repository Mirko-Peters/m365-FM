Many Azure tenants grow into cloud chaos: no naming convention, no policies, subscriptions nobody owns. This episode explains Azure landing zones in plain English for cloud architects and platform teams. You learn the eight design areas, the platform versus application split and how to start with a minimum viable landing zone.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – The cloud chaos problem
  • 00:48 – What an Azure landing zone actually is
  • 02:11 – Identity, billing and resource organization
  • 03:50 – Networking and security
  • 06:03 – Management, governance and automation
  • 08:31 – Platform vs. application landing zones
  • 10:32 – How the pieces connect
  • 13:12 – Start small: actionable takeaways

Key takeaways

  • An Azure landing zone is not a product but a pre-configured environment where the subscription is the unit of isolation and guardrails are in place before workloads arrive.
  • Policies assigned high in the management group hierarchy, combined with subscription vending, make every new subscription compliant, connected and monitored from the moment it exists.
  • Start with a minimum viable landing zone, turn on policies in audit mode first and plan IP ranges and the management group hierarchy before deploying anything.

Worth quoting

“The main idea is that the subscription is the unit of isolation, not the resource group. A subscription is your blast radius boundary.” – Mirko Peters (01:16)

“This is the difference between governance that's a wiki page and governance that's code. Teams move fast inside their box, but the platform team controls the box, not the contents.” – Mirko Peters (01:58)
