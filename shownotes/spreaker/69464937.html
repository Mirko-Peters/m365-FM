Many teams treat Microsoft Fabric lineage and Purview as governance, then learn in an audit that a diagram never stopped a single copy. This episode gives data architects and governance leads five realistic failure scenarios, a four-question litmus test and a 30 to 60 day plan to separate prevention from observability.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Governance is a verb, lineage is a noun
  • 03:27 – The missing policy enforcement point
  • 07:19 – Fabric is a router, not a firewall
  • 11:10 – Deterministic versus probabilistic security
  • 15:10 – Five inevitability scenarios
  • 38:00 – The responsibility map
  • 41:41 – Four-question litmus test and decision tree
  • 49:14 – A 30 to 60 day governance model

Key takeaways

  • Fabric lineage and Purview are observability tools that describe what happened; they cannot refuse an action before it executes.
  • Workspace roles, capacities and sensitivity labels allocate capability or describe data, so without a policy enforcement point Fabric security drifts from deterministic to probabilistic.
  • Test every control with four questions (can it say no, before execution, centrally, failing safely) and place prevention at controlled egress points outside Fabric.

Worth quoting

“Lineage tells you the system was moving fast and where it went. Governance is the thing that stops it from crossing the line in the first place.” – Mirko Peters (01:22)

“So the problem is not that lineage is bad. The problem is that lineage is being used as a substitute for authority. Lineage lives after the action. Governance lives before the action.” – Mirko Peters (03:13)
