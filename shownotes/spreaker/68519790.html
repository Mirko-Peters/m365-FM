3D models and digital twins break folder-based governance: rights, versions and lineage scatter across tools and regions. This episode shows data and platform teams how Microsoft Fabric governance with Entra ID, lineage, OneLake and rights-as-code keeps even real-time 3D assets compliant and traceable.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why 3D objects stress-test governance
  • 00:41 – Defining Fabric governance
  • 04:25 – Why 3D data breaks traditional systems
  • 08:00 – Versioning and provenance of digital twins
  • 11:28 – Interoperability and rights management
  • 14:43 – Governing real-time 3D assets
  • 19:50 – The future of digital trust

Key takeaways

  • 3D assets and digital twins are constellations of meshes, textures, scans and rights, which makes them the hardest test for Microsoft Fabric governance.
  • Entra ID identity, lineage, classification labels and OneLake shortcuts let one canonical asset serve many teams without brittle copies.
  • Version twin manifests, pin dependencies and treat licenses as machine-readable, versioned components enforced at access time with time-bound tokens.

Worth quoting

“Governance in fabric isn't a stack of policies you forget to enforce. It's the operating system for your data life, identity, permissioning, lineage, classification, policy, and monitoring, wired into one-lake, workspaces, items, and compute.” – Mirko Peters (00:45)

“Governance without lineage is theater. Fabric's built-in lineage maps how a raw capture flowed into a processed mesh into a compressed LOD set into a robot training simulation and finally into a KPI dashboard showing training efficiency.” – Mirko Peters (01:49)
