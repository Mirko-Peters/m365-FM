Many enterprise Azure environments have security and compliance policies on paper that the platform does not actually enforce. This episode is for cloud architects, platform teams and security leaders who need governance that scales across hundreds of subscriptions. You will learn how landing zones, management groups, RBAC with PIM, Azure Policy and continuous compliance work together as one enforced control plane.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Governance is enforced intent, not documentation
  • 04:36 – Governance by design: deterministic guardrails
  • 08:25 – Enterprise landing zones and management groups
  • 12:12 – Subscription strategy and blast radius boundaries
  • 16:06 – RBAC and Privileged Identity Management
  • 24:23 – Azure Policy as the resource state gate
  • 30:17 – Posture, continuous compliance and FinOps guardrails
  • 38:45 – The operating model and three enterprise scenarios

Key takeaways

  • Azure governance fails mainly through drift, so exceptions need owners, expiry dates and revalidation instead of living forever.
  • A shallow management group hierarchy, intent-based subscriptions, group-based RBAC and PIM give inheritance and access boundaries real meaning.
  • Azure Policy with deny, modify and deploy-if-not-exists is the gate, while Defender for Cloud, Compliance Manager and FinOps guardrails feed back into enforced rules.

Worth quoting

“Exceptions are not special cases. They are permanent forks in system behavior unless you design them to expire.” – Mirko Peters (04:25)
