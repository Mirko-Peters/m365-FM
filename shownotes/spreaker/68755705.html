An attacker can read your mail and files without ever touching a password, and MFA will not notice. This episode shows Microsoft 365 admins and security teams how illicit OAuth consent grants work, why resets do not revoke them, and which Entra controls, detections and remediation steps shut them down.

Key takeaways

  • OAuth consent grants give apps tokens that keep refreshing, so MFA and password resets do not cut off a malicious app; only revoking the grant does.
  • Lock down user consent to low-risk scopes, allow it only for verified publishers and route every risky request through the admin consent workflow.
  • Hunt for new grants, risky scopes such as offline access with mail or files permissions, and added app credentials in audit logs and via Microsoft Graph, then revoke and verify.

Worth quoting

“MFA protects authentication. Consent grants create tokens after a one-time dance. Those tokens keep refreshing with refreshed tokens.” – Mirko Peters (01:13)

“The simple version is authentication answers, who are you? Authorization answers, what can you do? This attack hijacks authorization.” – Mirko Peters (04:13)
