Your SharePoint structure may look perfect on paper while users still cannot find what they need. This episode gives Microsoft 365 admins and information managers a three-layer method to audit structure, usage and value, and to turn the results into a findability roadmap leadership will act on.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – How long does it take to find a file?
  • 00:48 – Why a perfect content map still fails
  • 03:53 – The three layers of content assessment
  • 07:23 – Separating signal from noise
  • 11:24 – Tracing user behavior to find gaps
  • 15:25 – Building a report leaders act on
  • 19:54 – Your next step

Key takeaways

  • A tidy SharePoint content map shows where files should live, not whether anyone finds or uses them.
  • A real assessment combines structural, behavioral and contextual evidence, using usage reports and search logs to separate active content from clutter.
  • Turn findings into a report with quick wins, medium-term projects, long-term governance commitments and named owners with KPIs.

Worth quoting

“Mapping catalogs what you have and where it sits. Assessment, on the other hand, asks whether those files still matter, who actually touches them, and how they fit into business workflows.” – Mirko Peters (02:11)

“Neat diagrams don't always point to value. A perfectly labeled collection of inactive files is still clutter, just with tidy labels.” – Mirko Peters (02:58)
