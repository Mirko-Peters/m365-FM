When a number in a report suddenly changes, most teams have no idea which hop in the pipeline caused it or who touched the data. This episode shows data and governance teams how Microsoft Fabric's lineage, permission inheritance, audit logs and Purview catalog turn the invisible middle of the Lakehouse into a map they can verify.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Seeing the invisible path data takes
  • 04:28 – One permission everywhere it matters
  • 08:44 – Audit logs as a single source of truth
  • 13:03 – Purview as the map room for your data
  • 17:25 – Transparency through every transformation

Key takeaways

  • Fabric captures lineage automatically as pipelines, Lakehouse tables, notebooks and reports are used, so the map stays current without manual documentation.
  • Workspaces act as the control point: permissions cascade from Lakehouse tables to Power BI datasets and reports, while audit logs tie every change to a user and object.
  • Purview extends this view across workspaces with a searchable catalog, classifications and business glossary terms, making lineage usable for audits and investigations.

Worth quoting

“With user driven documentation, it's only as current as the last time someone updated it, assuming they remember to update it at all. With Fabric, this happens as a side effect of using the platform.” – Mirko Peters (03:06)
