Copilot makes your teams faster, but are they still making good decisions? This episode is for leaders rolling out Microsoft 365 Copilot who want productivity gains without losing judgment, debate and ownership. You get a three-layer collaboration model and practical norms that keep humans accountable when AI is always in the room.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – AI as participant, not a feature
  • 04:45 – The three-layer collaboration model
  • 08:32 – Structural: meetings, chat and draft-first work
  • 20:17 – Cognitive: Copilot as co-author and authority bias
  • 32:21 – Work Graph, Work IQ and narrative power
  • 40:47 – Experiential: safety, ownership and Viva Insights
  • 57:02 – Intentional friction and a case study
  • 65:06 – Leadership rules and collaboration norms

Key takeaways

  • Copilot is not a feature toggle but a participant that frames decisions, so judgment, context and accountability become more valuable, not less.
  • AI drafts, recaps and summaries raise output while eroding shared understanding unless teams protect epistemic agency and treat recaps as input, not truth.
  • Intentional friction — human-written problem statements, two alternatives, decision logs and visible AI contribution — keeps decisions owned and humans irreplaceable.

Worth quoting

“Editing a draft is not the same as constructing the model of the problem in your head. Review optimizes for polish. Authorship optimizes for understanding.” – Mirko Peters (02:59)

“AI doesn't have to be right to become authoritative. It just has to be readable. Fluency is interpreted as competence.” – Mirko Peters (24:56)
