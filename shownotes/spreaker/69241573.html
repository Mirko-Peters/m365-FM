Fabric and Copilot make pipelines, notebooks and reports appear in minutes, yet many teams end up with capacity spikes, conflicting KPIs and unauditable access. This episode is for senior data engineers, architects and platform owners inheriting Fabric estates and explains the three failure modes plus an enforcement-first operating model that keeps cost, contracts and governance under control.

  • 27:12 – Case 2: lakehouse-warehouse contract collapse
  • 33:35 – Case 3: workspace-only security
  • 42:02 – The modern data engineer and operating model

Key takeaways

  • Fabric's single capacity and workspace model removes the friction that once slowed bad decisions, so cost, contract and access drift surface faster and louder.
  • Make views and stored procedures the only consumption surface and treat execution plans with bounded predicates as acceptance criteria to keep capacity spend predictable.
  • Enforce schemas, validation gates and quarantine tables at the lakehouse-to-warehouse seam and secure data with schema-based roles instead of workspace roles.
