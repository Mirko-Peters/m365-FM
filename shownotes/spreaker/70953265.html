Many Microsoft 365 and Power Platform solutions shine in a pilot and then fragment when they roll out across the enterprise. This episode is for architects, platform owners and transformation leaders who need their successes to survive scale. Learn the five failure modes behind the scaling paradox and a practical model for scaling principles instead of copying solutions.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why scale changes system behavior
  • 04:31 – The pilot that worked and the rollout that failed
  • 12:14 – Failure mode: tenant and workspace sprawl
  • 16:02 – Failure mode: data lineage gaps
  • 19:56 – Failure mode: role and ownership ambiguity
  • 23:40 – Environment chaos, hidden connectors and the governance trap
  • 51:03 – Principles for controlled scale
  • 70:29 – AI amplification and a practical scaling model

Key takeaways

  • A successful pilot proves usefulness, not scalability; enterprise scale requires ownership, lifecycle and decision logic to scale with the solution.
  • Workspace sprawl, data lineage gaps, ownership ambiguity, Power Platform environment chaos and hidden connectors are symptoms of one root cause: capacity that did not scale with demand.
  • Standardize the few controls that protect trust, allow bounded local adaptation, measure system health instead of adoption and fund capability before rollout waves.

Worth quoting

“A pilot proves usefulness. It does not prove scalability. Scalability only exists when the surrounding operating model can carry the solution under heavier load across more boundaries with less shared context and still produce trusted outcomes.” – Mirko Peters (04:15)

“Replication is about copying outputs. Scaling is about reproducing operating conditions. Those are not the same thing.” – Mirko Peters (08:37)
