Quantum computing sounds like physics homework, but developers can write Q# on a laptop today. This episode walks .NET and cloud developers through setup, a first Hello World with a Hadamard gate, statistical debugging on the simulator and submitting the same job to Azure Quantum hardware.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Quantum code isn't magic
  • 00:36 – Why quantum code feels familiar
  • 03:35 – Your quantum playground: setting up Q# and Azure Quantum
  • 07:10 – Writing your first quantum program
  • 11:05 – Debugging in a world where you can't peek
  • 14:55 – From laptop to quantum computer
  • 19:00 – Wrap-up

Key takeaways

  • Q# looks like a familiar language with namespaces, operations and types; the real shift is reasoning about qubits, probabilities, measurement and interference.
  • A working setup needs a current .NET SDK, the Quantum Development Kit with the Q# extension in VS Code, and an Azure subscription with a Quantum workspace linked through the Azure CLI.
  • Debug quantum programs statistically on the simulator with many shots and assertions, then treat deviations on real Azure Quantum hardware as physical noise rather than logic bugs.

Worth quoting
