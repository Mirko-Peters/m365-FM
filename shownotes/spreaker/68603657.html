Copilot sounds confident but falls short when a question depends on your own DLP exceptions, regional SOPs or compliance memos. This episode shows Microsoft 365 architects and developers how to build a custom engine agent and bring it into Copilot Chat with a small manifest change. You get a build, deploy and verify plan for faster, cited and more accurate answers.

In this episode

  • 11:05 – Build, deploy, verify
  • 16:39 – Before versus after: three proof cases
  • 20:17 – Life cycle, governance and scale

Key takeaways

  • Default Copilot is grounded in Microsoft Graph and public knowledge and cannot apply company-specific DLP rules, SOPs or compliance memos on its own.
  • A custom engine agent adds retrieval with Azure AI Search, actions against internal APIs and guardrails such as tenant controls and data scopes.
  • Updating the app manifest to schema 1.22 with the right Copilot agent capabilities brings the specialist into Copilot Chat, where it answers with citations.

Worth quoting

“Default Copilot doesn't know your exception list. It doesn't know your project Orion carve out. It can describe DLP in theory, it can't apply your DLP in practice. That gap is where incidents happen.” – Mirko Peters (01:17)

“Because out of the box, Copilot is optimized for breadth, not your depth. It's grounded by Microsoft Graph and Public Knowledge, which is the right default for most people, but the wrong default for enterprise precision.” – Mirko Peters (02:26)
