Many Microsoft 365 Copilot users chase a perfect one-shot prompt and get generic results. This episode shows experienced users how iteration, focused context and deliberate tone produce precise, business-ready output, and why newer models make that discipline more important.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why the perfect prompt is a myth
  • 00:39 – The myth of the perfect prompt
  • 03:56 – Iteration: the engineer's secret weapon
  • 07:20 – Context: the secret ingredient
  • 11:16 – Tone: the hidden lever
  • 14:55 – GPT-5: more brain, same rules
  • 18:22 – Build systems, not perfect prompts

Key takeaways

  • Copilot works best with iterative, layered prompts built on goal, context, expectations and sources rather than one oversized request.
  • Relevant context such as company values, vocabulary and templates, plus deliberate tone, makes Copilot output match your organization instead of generic filler.
  • Newer models like GPT-5 amplify both good and bad prompting habits, so verification and systematic prompting matter more than ever.

Worth quoting

“Iteration beats over engineering every single time. The perfect prompt doesn't exist, and pretending it does will only slow you down.” – Mirko Peters (03:37)

“Outputs should never be accepted at face value. Microsoft explicitly urges users to review and verify responses from copilot.” – Mirko Peters (06:27)
