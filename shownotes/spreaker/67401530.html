Duplicated Power Query scripts, parallel pipelines and reports that never agree are the daily cost of fragmented ETL. This episode is for data and BI teams moving to Microsoft Fabric who want to understand how Dataflows Gen2, with compute separation, managed staging and Git integration, replaces workarounds with one maintainable data flow.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – From ETL pain points to a unified Fabric
  • 04:43 – The core architecture blueprint
  • 08:58 – Seamless flow across Fabric
  • 13:05 – Resource control without the guesswork
  • 17:15 – Collaborating without collisions
  • 21:23 – Why Gen2 changes how data teams work

Key takeaways

  • Dataflows Gen2 makes transformations a first-class Fabric resource, so one definition feeds the lakehouse, warehouse and Power BI.
  • Compute separation and managed staging let teams scale transformation power on demand and stop paying for leftover temporary data.
  • Git integration with branches, reviews and commit history turns ETL into a collaborative, auditable engineering practice.

Worth quoting

“In Gen two, it exists once, and every connected service points to that canonical definition. If an upstream column changes, there's one place to update the logic, and every dependent service sees it immediately.” – Mirko Peters (03:40)

“The transformation logic executes in a dedicated compute layer, which you can scale up or down based on workload size without touching the underlying storage.” – Mirko Peters (06:24)
