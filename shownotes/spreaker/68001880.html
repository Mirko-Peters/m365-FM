If your team keeps arguing about lakehouse vs warehouse and copying the same datasets into five places, this episode gives you a plain-English mental model of Microsoft Fabric. Data and BI teams learn what OneLake, Delta Parquet, shortcuts and workspace permissions actually do, so they can cut duplicate storage and get consistent numbers.

  • 10:56 – Shortcuts: sharing without duplicating
  • 14:40 – Workspaces and governance
  • 18:14 – Wrap-up: one pool, fewer copies

Key takeaways

  • In Fabric, a warehouse is the SQL-first home for curated reporting, while a lakehouse is the flexible workbench for semi-structured data and Spark, and both sit on the same OneLake storage.
  • OneLake stores tables in the open Delta Parquet format, which combines columnar efficiency with ACID transactions, schema enforcement and time travel so SQL and Spark engines can work on the same files.
  • Shortcuts let teams reference data in other workspaces or external stores without copying it, but because SQL access can run under the shortcut creator's identity, workspace roles, item permissions and shortcut creators need governance.

Worth quoting

“The tools are designed to coexist, not to substitute for each other. So the bottom line, warehouses serve reporting, lakehouses serve engineering and exploration.” – Mirko Peters (02:52)

“One Lake gives you the centralized foundation, but it relies on the Delta format layer to keep data clean, consistent, and usable across different engines.” – Mirko Peters (06:42)
