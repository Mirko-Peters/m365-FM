Most Teams notifications are static FYI pings that nobody acts on. This episode shows Microsoft 365 admins and makers how to combine Microsoft Lists, Adaptive Cards and Power Automate into actionable notifications that users can approve or snooze without leaving chat.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why your Teams notifications are dumb
  • 00:35 – Why Teams notifications fail
  • 04:21 – The secret weapon: Microsoft Lists
  • 07:42 – Designing your first Adaptive Card
  • 11:28 – Power Automate: wiring it all together
  • 15:05 – Making people actually click
  • 18:20 – Three steps to working notifications

Key takeaways

  • Static Teams notifications get ignored because they carry no action, while Adaptive Cards bring approve, snooze or update buttons straight into the chat.
  • Clean, typed columns in Microsoft Lists are the schema that makes cards clear, and card fields should map directly to list column names.
  • Wire cards with Power Automate using targeted triggers, a verified Teams connector and a pilot group, and keep each card to one or two actions.

Worth quoting

“Notifications don't have to be treated like paper slips shoved under a digital door. They can ask for action directly. They can carry buttons, fields, and contexts so users can respond instantly.” – Mirko Peters (03:24)

“Lists aren't passive storage. They are the schema, the definition of what your workflow actually means. Every column sets a rule, every field enforces structure.” – Mirko Peters (07:10)
