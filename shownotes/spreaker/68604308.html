HR notifications, offer letters and policy updates sent through service accounts break at the next MFA prompt or password expiry and leave an unclear audit trail. This episode shows Power Platform and Microsoft 365 admins how to move email flows to an app registration, Microsoft Graph and application access policies, so every message is scoped, reliable and provable.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why service accounts sabotage HR email flows
  • 02:40 – Non-human identities: app registration plus Graph
  • 08:45 – Locking down sending with application access policies
  • 13:50 – Graph sendMail payloads, retries and idempotency
  • 18:50 – Building a governed Power Automate custom connector
  • 22:50 – Auditing, monitoring and alerting
  • 25:40 – Runbooks, ownership and closing the gap

Key takeaways

  • Service accounts with shared passwords, Send As rights and MFA exemptions make Power Automate email flows fragile, over-privileged and impossible to audit cleanly.
  • An app registration with Mail.Send application permission, authenticated via client credentials and fenced by an Exchange Online application access policy, can only send from approved mailboxes.
  • Wrapping the Graph call in a custom connector with client request IDs, normalized errors, throttling-aware retries and idempotency keys gives every flow traceable, reliable delivery.

Worth quoting

“Service accounts sabotage your flows because they conflate human authentication with machine tasks, invite over privilege, erase attribution, and collapse the second conditional access evolves.” – Mirko Peters (03:56)

“Client credentials flows aren't subject to user MFA. That's not a loophole, it's the design. You enforce safety at the permission layer and the policy fence, not with interactive challenges.” – Mirko Peters (06:50)
