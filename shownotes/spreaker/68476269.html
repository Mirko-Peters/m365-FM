Many Power Apps start on SharePoint lists because they are included and quick to set up. This episode is for makers and IT decision makers who want to understand why those apps stall at a few thousand records, break their lookups and pile up technical debt. You will learn where SharePoint's limits are and when Dataverse or SQL is the better foundation.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – The SharePoint-as-database myth
  • 01:00 – What a database actually is
  • 04:05 – The scale myth and delegation
  • 10:18 – Lookups and broken relationships
  • 15:54 – Security, lifecycle and technical debt
  • 20:18 – The licensing trap
  • 21:24 – Filing cabinets are not engines

Key takeaways

  • SharePoint lists are built for collaboration and documents, not for transactional, relational workloads at scale.
  • Limited delegation, lookup columns without referential integrity and item-level permissions make Power Apps on SharePoint slow down sharply as data grows.
  • When an app needs scale, concurrency and integrity, move the data to Dataverse or SQL and budget for it instead of relying on the free option.

Worth quoting

“SharePoint was not designed to manage transactions at scale. Its DNA is collaboration, version, history, permissions, and file storage.” – Mirko Peters (01:40)

“Power apps can connect to SharePoint, but that's all it does. Connect. It borrows the data source, but it doesn't make SharePoint any smarter.” – Mirko Peters (02:47)
