Many Microsoft 365 tenants have a few blanket DLP rules that tick a compliance box while sensitive files still leak through Teams, OneDrive and email. This episode is for admins and security owners who want real protection, walking through discovery, classification, policy design and monitoring as one layered DLP strategy.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Mapping where sensitive data lives
  • 04:18 – Classification: deciding what really matters
  • 08:35 – Policy definition: conditions, actions and scope
  • 12:53 – Monitoring, reporting and tuning
  • 16:34 – DLP as a living fortress
  • 20:52 – Test your policies against real actions

Key takeaways

  • Start DLP with discovery: Content Explorer and Activity Explorer show where sensitive information types actually sit across SharePoint, OneDrive, Teams and Exchange.
  • Classify with nuance using built-in and custom sensitive information types plus a mix of auto and manual labeling, because blanket restrictions drive users to workarounds.
  • Treat DLP as a living system: combine targeted conditions and actions with policy tips, monitor matches and false positives, and revisit discovery regularly.

Worth quoting

“If every file is protected with the same heavy set of restrictions, users stop trusting the system. They'll find workarounds or worse, ignore the rules outright. That's not security, it's friction disguised as control.” – Mirko Peters (04:48)

“At its core, a DLP policy has two main parts, conditions and actions. Conditions look for what's inside the data or how it's being moved. Actions decide what to do with that information.” – Mirko Peters (09:18)
