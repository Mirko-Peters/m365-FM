Think AI compliance is Microsoft's or OpenAI's problem? Under the EU AI Act, deployers share the obligations. This episode helps IT, compliance and data protection leads compare Copilot and ChatGPT, understand the risk ladder and set up the checklist auditors will actually ask for.

In this episode

  • 04:09 – Copilot: compliance built into the furniture
  • 08:11 – ChatGPT: flexibility meets bureaucracy
  • 12:55 – Practical survival guide for deployers
  • 17:32 – The Act is not an innovation killer
  • 21:44 – Conclusion and three next steps

Key takeaways

  • Under the EU AI Act, a system's risk class depends on how and where you use it, so the same generative tool can move from limited to high risk.
  • Copilot inherits Microsoft 365 governance such as tenant boundaries, Purview and audit logs, while standalone ChatGPT leaves deployers to build those safeguards themselves.
  • Deployers still need a DPIA and processing records, data classification and DLP, retained audit logs and role-based AI literacy training.

Worth quoting

“Every AI system gets ranked into one of four categories unacceptable, high, limited, or minimal. That box isn't cosmetic. It dictates the exact compliance weights strapped to you” – Mirko Peters (00:55)

“But classification isn't about what the system looks like. It's about what it does and in what context you use it. Minimal doesn't mean permanent free pass.” – Mirko Peters (01:16)
