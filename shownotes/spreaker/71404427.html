Copilot does not break your permissions, it simply makes every overshared file instantly findable. This episode is for IT, security and compliance leaders planning or stuck in a Copilot rollout, and it shows how a Microsoft Purview baseline of labels, adaptive scopes, prompt-level DLP and audit turns that exposure into controlled adoption.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Copilot as the unauthorized auditor
  • 00:49 – Security through obscurity breaks
  • 03:47 – The governance gap before scale
  • 07:21 – Three failure patterns
  • 11:17 – Purview: build the perimeter around context
  • 15:21 – Expanded DLP enforcement in 2026
  • 18:33 – Audit, tuning and closed-loop governance

Key takeaways

  • Copilot does not create new access; it removes the friction that used to hide overshared content, so permission hygiene must come before scale.
  • Microsoft Purview works best label-first: sensitivity labels, adaptive scopes and prompt-level DLP let policy follow the meaning of content instead of its location.
  • Audit data closes the loop, letting teams prove safe usage, investigate incidents and tune policies as Copilot usage patterns change.
