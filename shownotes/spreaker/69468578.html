Most Power Platform failures start at the data layer, when Dataverse is treated like a nicer place to store rows and the first app shapes the model. This episode, inspired by Bülent Altinsoy's Dataverse workshop, is for makers, architects and leaders who want to understand why smart data models, relationships, security and ALM decide whether apps, audits and AI agents scale or drift.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why low-code data keeps failing in production
  • 04:44 – Dataverse as a business semantics engine
  • 08:42 – Why data modeling is a leadership topic
  • 12:59 – Tables, columns and relationships
  • 21:01 – Solutions and environments: delivery as architecture
  • 24:43 – From SharePoint to Dataverse and audit readiness
  • 32:42 – The AI moment and agents that need memory
  • 40:56 – One model, many apps, and the anti-patterns

Key takeaways

  • Dataverse is a business semantics engine, so tables, columns and relationships are commitments about what the business believes is true, not UI scaffolding.
  • Data modeling is leadership work: name and own the core business concepts, design relationship behavior deliberately, and protect the model with environments and managed solutions.
  • AI and agents consume whatever meaning the model makes explicit, so ownership, security roles, descriptions and relationships become the foundation for reliable grounding and agent memory.

Worth quoting

“A database stores facts. Dataverse stores facts plus meaning, relationships, ownership, security boundaries, metadata, and life cycle behavior that travel with the data across apps, automation, and increasingly AI.” – Mirko Peters (04:58)

“If you want a simple leadership test for Dataverse, ask who owns the model. Not who built the app, who owns the model.” – Mirko Peters (12:24)
