Exporting Power BI dashboards to PDF produces broken, unreliable printouts. This episode helps Power BI developers and report owners choose between the Power BI service builder, Power BI Report Builder and Visual Studio Reporting Services projects to deliver pixel-perfect paginated reports.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why Power BI dashboards fail on paper
  • 00:30 – Why paginated reports exist
  • 02:30 – Way one: Power BI service web paginated builder
  • 05:55 – Way two: Power BI Report Builder
  • 11:00 – Way three: Visual Studio with Reporting Services projects
  • 15:45 – Decision matrix: pick the right tool
  • 18:08 – Techniques and best practices for paginated reports

Key takeaways

  • Power BI dashboards are built for screens; paginated reports based on RDL are the right tool for fixed, print-ready, audit-proof output.
  • Use the web paginated builder in the Power BI service for quick tabular prototypes and Power BI Report Builder for real page control, charts and parameters.
  • Move to Visual Studio with Reporting Services projects when you need report suites with shared data sets, source control, CI/CD and governed deployments.

Worth quoting

“Decide early. Dashboards are for screens, paginated is for paper. If the primary output goes to a printer, jump tools before you waste a sprint polishing a dashboard that will always betray you in the copy room.” – Mirko Peters (02:09)

“Build a one page prototype here before committing to deeper tooling. Use it as a live spec, align on paper size, headers, footers, and grouping with your stakeholder.” – Mirko Peters (05:10)
