Why do some enterprise applications become impossible to change while others keep evolving for years? .NET architect Miguel Castro explains how extensibility, plug-in hooks and the strategy pattern keep systems maintainable, drawing on lessons from ASP.NET and a real live-transcription platform. A practical conversation for developers and architects who want software that survives change.

Key takeaways

  • Enterprise systems become hard to maintain mainly because they were not designed to allow enhancement and modification, which is exactly what extensibility addresses.
  • Extensible designs rest on abstraction: strategy-style interfaces, plug-in hooks and dependency injection let parts be swapped without touching the core pipeline.
  • AI can speed up writing small components, but architecture and design based on client requirements still need a human architect.
