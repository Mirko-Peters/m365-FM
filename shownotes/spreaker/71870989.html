AI-written emails and cloned voices make fraudulent payment requests look perfectly legitimate, and classic email authentication cannot stop them. This episode is for security architects, identity teams and finance leaders who need a stronger defense against deepfake business email compromise. Learn how Microsoft Entra Verified ID moves trust from the message to provable authority at the point of decision.

Key takeaways

  • SPF, DKIM and DMARC prove how an email was sent and handled, but they cannot prove that the sender has authority for the requested action.
  • Microsoft Entra Verified ID lets workflows require a signed, verifiable credential that proves specific authority at the moment of a high-risk action.
  • Design narrow, quickly revocable credentials around decision rights and start with one high-risk workflow such as treasury approvals or executive help desk resets.

Worth quoting

“Mail authentication proves that a server was authorized to send a message, and it proves how that message was handled during transport. But it does not prove that the sender has actual authority.” – Mirko Peters (03:07)

“Identity tells you who the person is, but authority tells you what that person is allowed to do. Those are not the same thing.” – Mirko Peters (13:48)
