Power Apps built on SharePoint lists often stall at 2,000 records, show blue delegation warnings and slow down as permissions grow. This episode gives makers and IT leaders the architectural reasons, measurable red flags, cost arguments and a migration path for deciding when an app belongs on Dataverse.

Key takeaways

  • SharePoint lists are built for content and collaboration, so Power Apps on top of them hit delegation limits, the 5,000-item list view threshold and slow item-level security.
  • Dataverse runs filters, joins, aggregations and row- and field-level security on the server, so apps stay correct and fast as data grows.
  • Move to Dataverse when lists exceed about 100,000 items, need compound filters, multiple lookups, offline or granular auditing, or when blue delegation banners appear in core screens.

Worth quoting

“SharePoint was built for content and collaboration. Documents, pages, conversations, basic lists. It's excellent at that. But a Power App is a data application. It needs a data platform that speaks relationships, delegation, and server-side execution.” – Mirko Peters (00:38)

“When Power Apps can't delegate a query, it pulls data to the client and evaluates locally. That's fast in demos and catastrophic at scale.” – Mirko Peters (00:55)
