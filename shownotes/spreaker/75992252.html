Many sensitivity label rollouts look green on the dashboard while protecting almost nothing. This episode is for information protection owners, Purview admins and compliance teams who want a practical path from pilot to production, covering taxonomy, encryption, defaults, auto-labeling, container labels and Copilot readiness.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why most labeling programs quietly fail
  • 05:05 – What a sensitivity label is and the executive case
  • 11:27 – Taxonomy design and a starter label set
  • 20:32 – Encryption, permissions and client compatibility
  • 30:32 – Designing and reading the pilot
  • 37:02 – Defaults, mandatory labeling and auto-labeling
  • 50:28 – Container labels, Conditional Access and adoption
  • 63:03 – Copilot readiness, governance and the roadmap

Key takeaways

  • Sensitivity labels fail on rollout design, not tooling: keep four or five plain-language labels that describe handling rules rather than departments, and test them against real documents.
  • Let the system do most of the classifying with defaults, light recommendations and auto-labeling run in simulation first, and reserve encryption for the top tier after testing a client compatibility matrix.
  • Copilot surfaces whatever users can already reach, so fix oversharing first, label the highest-risk stores, pilot with a clean group and measure coverage, correctness and protected content.
