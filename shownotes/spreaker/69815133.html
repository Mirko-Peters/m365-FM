Many organizations buy Microsoft Fabric expecting one platform with one governance model, then watch costs drift and trust erode. This episode is for data platform owners, architects and FinOps leads who need Fabric governance that changes runtime behavior instead of producing documentation. You will learn why workspaces, domains and capacity metrics fall short and which enforced controls actually stop the decay.

Key takeaways

  • Fabric capacities, workspaces, domains and OneLake are shared pools and organizing labels, not enforced boundaries, so governance has to be stronger than these abstractions.
  • Fabric cost drifts with sprawl, duplication and background load even when visible usage looks stable, and chargeback only works when cost is tied to an owner who can retire assets.
  • Real governance works like an authorization compiler: enforced creation rules, defaults, promotion gates, retirement and automation, so that nothing exists without an owner, a purpose and an end date.
