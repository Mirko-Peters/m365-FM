Many organizations treat AI as just another Azure workload and only discover runaway cost, misfiring agents and ungoverned identities after the fact. This episode gives CIOs, CTOs, CISOs and CFOs five inevitability scenarios, the architecture questions that matter and a 30-day review agenda to put enforceable constraints in place before deployment.

  • 31:35 – AI gravity and unplanned lock-in
  • 39:15 – Governance after the fact and audit failure
  • 47:00 – Executive questions and the 30-day review agenda

Key takeaways

  • AI is a probabilistic decision engine running on deterministic Azure infrastructure, so autoscale, retries and monitoring can amplify uncertainty instead of containing it.
  • Cost, action and identity need deny-before-execute choke points: cost classes and hard limits, approval gates for state-changing actions, and separate identities for execution and decision.
  • Leaders should decide what must stay portable, move governance from after-the-fact visibility to pre-execution enforcement, and run a 30-day review that produces an owned constraint map.

Worth quoting

“A workload is something you host. A decision engine is something you constrain. If you keep treating AI like another workload, the outcome is inevitable.” – Mirko Peters (03:53)
