Is your Power BI report crawling every time someone adds a filter? This episode is for report authors and data modelers fighting tangled tables and fragile DAX. Learn how a clean star schema, a proper date table and human-readable slicers make models fast and trustworthy.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – From digital spaghetti to a star schema
  • 00:55 – The digital spaghetti problem
  • 04:10 – Facts versus dimensions
  • 07:59 – Normalize the fact, flatten the dimension
  • 11:39 – The sacred date table
  • 15:00 – Beating blanks, flags and cryptic codes
  • 18:45 – Wrap-up: three rules to remember

Key takeaways

  • A single flat table forces Power BI's engine to scan duplicated text on every query, while a star schema with slim fact tables and descriptive dimension tables keeps filters fast and correct.
  • Normalize facts with surrogate keys and a single grain, and flatten dimensions instead of building snowflake chains.
  • A dedicated date table with role-playing copies, plus cleaned-up blanks, flags and codes in Power Query, makes time intelligence reliable and slicers trustworthy.

Worth quoting

“Facts are the measurements how many, how much, how often? Dimensions are the descriptions, the who, what, when and where.” – Mirko Peters (04:29)

“If you want a mental image, facts are the receipts. Dimensions are the catalog. Receipts don't carry full product names, addresses, or job titles. They just reference IDs and amounts” – Mirko Peters (06:45)
