Azure workloads that look healthy in the portal can still fail during payroll runs or Monday-morning logins. This episode helps architects and engineers understand why solutions break under real load, what downtime really costs, and how to design and test resilient, highly available systems.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why Azure breaks when you need it most
  • 03:55 – The hidden cost of downtime
  • 06:26 – Five pillars of resilient Azure design
  • 10:29 – Azure tools that stop common failures
  • 14:29 – Resilience in the real world

Key takeaways

  • Most Azure outages under load come from design shortcuts, weak autoscale rules and hidden dependencies rather than from the platform itself.
  • Resilient workloads rest on five pillars: availability, redundancy, elasticity, observability and security, built in from day one rather than added after an incident.
  • Site Recovery, Traffic Manager, availability zones, Azure Monitor and Chaos Studio only help when they are enabled, configured and tested under realistic conditions.
