Copilot does not browse your SharePoint navigation; it retrieves whatever a user can reach and whatever matches the prompt. This episode is for SharePoint architects, information managers, Microsoft 365 admins and Copilot rollout teams. You learn how sites, hubs, permissions, labels, metadata and lifecycle policies combine into an information architecture that keeps Copilot answers safe and correct.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why navigation was the security boundary
  • 03:14 – Context replaces navigation: how Copilot reads your tenant
  • 09:16 – Failure modes: oversharing and wrong answers
  • 21:51 – Sites, hubs and permissions as architecture
  • 37:47 – Sharing defaults and restricted access controls
  • 44:19 – Sensitivity labels and metadata
  • 61:51 – Site lifecycle, cleanup and governance operations
  • 74:33 – Testing Copilot and leadership decisions

Key takeaways

  • Copilot respects permissions exactly as they are set, so it surfaces every old company-wide link, open group and broken inheritance that obscurity used to hide.
  • Measure exposure first, then design sites by purpose and audience with group-based permissions, safe sharing defaults, sensitivity labels and shared content types so the right file wins retrieval.
  • Treat governance as operations: give every site owners and an end date, automate inactive and ownerless site policies, archive stale content and test Copilot with a recurring prompt set.

Worth quoting

“Copilot doesn't bypass permissions. It respects them exactly as they're set. If a document is locked down to five people, the sixth person's copilot can't pull it.” – Mirko Peters (07:30)

“A hub doesn't grant access to anything. If you associate a site with a hub, nobody gains the right to open it. Nobody loses a right either.” – Mirko Peters (28:26)
