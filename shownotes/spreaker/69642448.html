Microsoft promised to be carbon negative, yet its emissions rose as AI and data centers scaled, which makes auditing its footprint a real governance problem. This episode is for sustainability leads, cloud architects and Microsoft 365 admins who want to understand the internal carbon fee, scope 3 accounting, carbon removals and Cloud for Sustainability, and how to apply a carbon-per-workload review in their own tenant.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – CSR as a governance system under constraint
  • 08:09 – The fraud boundary: marketing versus measurable impact
  • 12:21 – Microsoft's commitments and what carbon negative means
  • 20:31 – Artifact one: the internal carbon fee
  • 29:10 – Emissions growth and carbon removal procurement
  • 37:48 – Case studies: cloud consumption, AI energy and data center limits
  • 51:30 – Cloud for Sustainability and Power Platform sprawl
  • 65:12 – Trade-offs, lessons and a one-week carbon review

Key takeaways

  • Corporate social responsibility only becomes real when it is measurable, budgeted and enforced through procurement, architecture and finance decisions.
  • Microsoft's internal carbon fee, carbon accounting across scopes 1 to 3, removal procurement and Cloud for Sustainability form a carbon control plane, but AI-driven growth makes the audit a moving target.
  • Organizations can copy the mechanics, not the slogans: treat carbon as a non-functional requirement, review one workload with one owner, and set region and runtime defaults that make waste expensive.

Worth quoting

“CSR becomes real only when it becomes measurable. The moment you can measure something, you can budget it. The moment you can budget it, you can enforce it.” – Mirko Peters (02:04)

“If sustainability lives in a report, it's optional. If sustainability lives in a budget line, it's real. The moment carbon has an internal price, engineers feel it without being asked to care.” – Mirko Peters (07:13)
