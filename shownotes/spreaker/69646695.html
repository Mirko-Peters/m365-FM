Many organizations roll out Copilot, measure adoption and quietly let plausible AI output stand in for human decisions. This episode is for leaders, architects and Microsoft 365 teams who want AI to scale capability instead of confusion, and shows how to put judgment, ownership and enforcement back into the workflow.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why tool metaphors fail for Copilot
  • 02:57 – Defining cognitive collaboration
  • 06:57 – AI scales ambiguity faster than capability
  • 15:05 – Mental models to unlearn
  • 28:51 – The triad: cognition, action, judgment
  • 33:13 – Scenarios: security, HR and IT change
  • 45:56 – Skills AI makes more valuable
  • 67:26 – Prescriptions that remove deniability

Key takeaways

  • Copilot is a probabilistic cognition engine, so its output must be treated as a proposal that a named human accepts or rejects, not as an answer.
  • More prompts, prompt libraries and delayed training cannot replace clear intent, explicit constraints, decision rights and governance that is actually enforced.
  • Decision logs, judgment moments embedded in workflows and named individual decision owners connect AI cognition to accountable action.

Worth quoting

“With deterministic tools, the human decides, and the tool executes. With cognitive systems, the tool proposes, and the human must decide.” – Mirko Peters (02:24)

“AI doesn't scale competence first. It scales whatever is already true in your environment. If your data is messy, it scales mess.” – Mirko Peters (07:02)
