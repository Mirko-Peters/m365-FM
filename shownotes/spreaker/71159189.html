Most Microsoft 365 governance relies on people remembering policies under pressure, and Copilot scales every gap that creates. This episode is for governance, security and IT leaders who want to replace fragile written rules with structural controls like default labels, adaptive DLP and clean permissions, and measure whether they actually work.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – The policy illusion
  • 04:13 – Why policies are not code
  • 08:01 – Dynamic systems and speed over policy
  • 15:52 – Compliance fatigue and desire paths
  • 28:36 – Fragile governance vs structural resilience
  • 32:46 – Pavement levers: labels, DLP, Copilot guardrails
  • 52:02 – Over-permissioning and governance signals
  • 65:34 – System design thinking and the operating model

Key takeaways

  • Written policies only express intent; real control comes from defaults, permissions, labels and DLP that execute at the moment of action.
  • Repeated workarounds and shadow IT are design feedback that show where the governed path is slower or harder than the unofficial one.
  • Track signals such as Copilot relevance, blocked versus nudged actions and workaround volume, and adjust the environment in a monthly loop.

Worth quoting

“If you remember nothing else from this section, remember that a policy is a statement of intent, while code is an execution mechanism.” – Mirko Peters (07:08)

“The single point of failure in this architecture is user memory. And memory is a terrible place to store a control.” – Mirko Peters (05:04)
