Copilot gives generic answers because it cannot see the wikis, old CRMs and ticketing systems where your real knowledge lives. This episode is for Microsoft 365 developers, admins and architects who want Copilot to understand their business. Learn when to use plugins or custom integrations and why Graph connectors are usually the best way to bring legacy data into Copilot securely.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Copilot's blind spot: your legacy data
  • 00:28 – Why Copilot misses the mark with your data
  • 05:12 – Three ways to make Copilot smarter: connectors, plugins, custom data
  • 10:03 – How Graph connectors bridge internal wikis and ancient CRMs
  • 15:34 – Business impact and real ROI
  • 20:44 – Where to start: connect one old system

Key takeaways

  • Out of the box, Copilot only sees content inside Microsoft 365, while much of an organization's knowledge lives in legacy wikis, CRMs and file shares.
  • Plugins add narrow, specific skills, custom data integrations need ongoing development, and Graph connectors broaden what Copilot can see at scale.
  • Graph connectors index external data while honoring existing permissions, so Copilot can answer business questions without risky data migrations.

Worth quoting

“Where plugins are like adding gadgets, graph connectors are more like knocking down the walls so Copilot can finally see the entire room.” – Mirko Peters (06:49)

“Plugins are best when you need a specific integration, like plugging into a critical service with a defined API, but not much else.” – Mirko Peters (09:07)
