PowerPoint Live is the way to present slides in a Teams meeting without sharing your whole screen. Teams loads the deck, shows you the slides with your notes, and lets attendees move through the slides at their own pace. Use it when the audience may be on slow connections, needs captions or translation, or wants to look back.

This guide follows two Microsoft Support pages on PowerPoint Live. They differ in small wording, such as **Share** versus **Share content**, and where that matters it is noted. They do not cover co-presenters or give guidance for external participants beyond the anonymous participant limit.

## What it does

According to Microsoft Support, PowerPoint Live combines PowerPoint features with Teams collaboration tools:

- The presenter gets one view with slides, speaker notes, participants, reactions, raised hands and chat.
- Attendees can navigate independently, translate slides, switch to high contrast and zoom.
- Viewing uses less bandwidth than typical screen sharing, which helps on weak connections.

It applies to Teams and PowerPoint for Microsoft 365 on Windows and Mac. Presenting from PowerPoint for the web is also described.

## How to start

### From the Teams meeting

1. Select **Share** (or **Share content**) in the meeting controls.
2. In the **PowerPoint Live** section, select your file. The list shows your recently opened or edited files from the team's SharePoint site or your OneDrive.
3. If the file is missing, select **Browse OneDrive** or **Browse my computer**.

### From PowerPoint

- In PowerPoint for Windows or Mac, open the file and select **Present in Teams** at the top.
- In PowerPoint for the web, select **Share content** > **Present in Teams**.

Microsoft's tip: finish editing a few minutes before presenting, and start a few minutes early so content can preload. If you change the deck after sharing starts, select **Present the latest version**.

## Presenter controls

- Move with the arrows or the thumbnail strip. **Grid view** shows all slides so you can jump.
- Use **Laser pointer**, **Pen**, **Highlighter** and **Eraser** to mark up slides.
- Select **Chat** to read messages while presenting.
- Select **Layout** to change what the audience sees.
- Use **Turn off participant navigation** to keep everyone on your slide. One page names this **Private view** in meeting controls.

### Presenter view

Presenter view shows slides with notes plus audience videos, raised hands and reactions. It is hidden by default on small-screen devices. Turn it on with **More options** > **Show presenter view** (one page says **Change view** > **Show presenter view**), or select the sharing window and press **Ctrl+Shift+X**. In Teams for the web, presenter view needs Microsoft Edge 18 or later, or Google Chrome 65 or later.

## What attendees can do

If the presenter allows it, attendees can:

- Move through slides without affecting the live view, then select **Sync to Presenter** to return.
- Jump with **Grid view** and select hyperlinks.
- Select **Change view** > **Translate slides** or **View slides in high contrast**.
- Use captions and a screen reader.
- Zoom with pinch, the plus and minus keys or Ctrl and scroll, pan with arrow keys or drag, and press **Esc** to reset. This does not change anyone else's view.

## What happens to the file

- Files listed under PowerPoint Live can be viewed by participants during the meeting. Their file access outside the meeting does not change.
- A file chosen with Browse that is not already in Teams is uploaded. In a channel meeting it goes to the channel's **Files** tab, where all team members can access it. In a private meeting it goes to your OneDrive, where only meeting participants can access it.

Check where your deck lands before presenting confidential content in a channel meeting.

## Requirements and limits

| Item | Value in Microsoft's pages |
|---|---|
| File types | .pptx and .ppsx (not .ppt, .pps, .odp, .pot) |
| File size | Up to 2 GB |
| Attendees | Up to 1,000 |
| Not supported | Teams live events, CVI devices, VTC devices |
| Presenting | Anonymous participants cannot present |
| Recording | Videos, animations and annotations are not captured |
| GCC tenants | Allow *.osi.office365.us and *.cdn.office365.us on port 443 |

Only the 2 GB size and 1,000 attendee numbers appear on one of the two pages, so confirm them on the current page if they matter.

## Tips from Microsoft's pages

- Use only one audio or video file per slide, because several files can play at once.
- Avoid animations on the final slide, since attendees may not see them after the presentation ends.
- Embedded Microsoft Stream videos are subject to SharePoint Online throttling limits.
- If you need a recording with animations, remember the recording does not capture them.

## When screen sharing is better

Because videos, animations and annotation marks are not in recordings, and live events are not supported, consider a normal window share for those cases. Microsoft's pages do not give a formal recommendation, so this is a practical reading of the limits above.

## Quick checklist before the meeting

1. File saved as .pptx or .ppsx, under 2 GB.
2. Deck stored where participants will not need more access than you intend.
3. One media file per slide, no animation on the last slide.
4. Decide whether to allow independent navigation.
5. Open the file a few minutes early.
