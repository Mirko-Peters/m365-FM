AB-731 is a business-oriented AI exam. It tests whether a leader can recognize AI opportunities, choose among Microsoft's AI tools, and plan adoption. This guide summarizes the skills outline from the Microsoft study guide. It contains no exam questions. The study guide gives no price, duration or retake information, and this guide does not use the exam page.

## About the guide

- Skills measured as of July 22, 2026.
- The page metadata lists an update on 2026-11-14.
- No retirement note appears on the page.
- The audience profile: candidates understand how to recognize opportunities for AI transformation, identify the right AI tools and resources, plan for AI adoption, optimize business processes and drive innovation with Microsoft 365 Copilot and Foundry Tools. The certification is designed for business decision-makers at all levels. They are not expected to write any code. They need experience leading adoption or change management and familiarity with Microsoft 365 services, Microsoft Foundry and general AI capabilities.

## Skills at a glance

| Area | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Identify the business value of generative AI solutions | 35-40% |
| Identify benefits, capabilities and opportunities for Microsoft's AI apps and services | 35-40% |
| Identify an implementation and adoption strategy for Microsoft's AI apps and services | 20-25% |

## Business value of generative AI (35-40%)

- **Foundations:** differences between generative AI and other types of AI, selecting a solution for a business need, pretrained and fine-tuned models, cost drivers including tokens and ROI, challenges such as fabrications, reliability and bias, and when generative AI gives business value.
- **Benefits and capabilities:** prompt engineering, grounding requirements, retrieval-augmented generation, the impact of data type and quality, secure AI, when machine learning adds value, the machine learning lifecycle, and security considerations for AI systems.

## Microsoft's AI apps and services (35-40%)

- **Microsoft 365 Copilot and Microsoft Copilot:** mapping processes and use cases, differences between versions, Copilot Chat on web and mobile, Copilot in Microsoft 365 apps, Copilot Studio, Microsoft Graph, integrated solutions with risk mitigation and safety benefits, when to use Researcher or Analyst, and when to build, buy or extend.
- **Foundry Tools:** mapping processes and use cases, capabilities including Azure Vision in Foundry Tools, Azure AI Search and Microsoft Foundry, matching a model to a business need, and benefits such as scalability and security.

## Implementation and adoption (20-25%)

- **Responsible AI:** its importance, governance principles, an AI council for strategy, oversight and alignment, and standards of fairness, reliability, safety, privacy, security, inclusiveness, transparency and accountability.
- **Adoption planning:** an adoption team, common barriers, an AI champions program, impacts on data, security, privacy and cost, Copilot license types (pay-as-you-go, monthly, and included with a Microsoft 365 subscription) and Foundry Tools subscription models (pay-as-you-go and commitment tiers).

## Naming note

Microsoft updated the product name Microsoft 365 Copilot to Microsoft Copilot. During the transition some exam questions may still say Microsoft 365 Copilot. Unless a question refers to licensing, read it as Microsoft Copilot. The Microsoft 365 Copilot name remains valid for the license.

## Change log

Compared with the version before July 22, 2026, the log marks the audience profile, Foundry Tools and plan for AI adoption as Minor. The two group-level entries it lists are No change, and the table lists no other rows.

## Scoring and practice

A score of 700 or greater is required to pass. Most questions cover generally available features, and Preview features may appear. Localized versions follow about eight weeks after the English version. If the exam is not in your language, you can request an additional 30 minutes. The new Practice Assessment is on AI Skills Navigator. The legacy assessment is no longer updated.

## A study plan

This plan is my advice, built on the weights.

1. Give most time to the first two areas, since they carry 70 to 80 percent together.
2. For each Copilot capability in the outline, write one business use case in your own words.
3. Learn the cost drivers for generative AI, especially tokens.
4. Prepare a short adoption plan with a team, champions and barriers.
5. Take the practice assessment and review weak areas.

## Typical mistakes (my advice)

- Studying technical implementation details that a non-coding leader exam does not ask for.
- Ignoring license types and subscription models in the adoption area.
- Getting confused by the old and new Copilot names.
- Skipping responsible AI principles.
