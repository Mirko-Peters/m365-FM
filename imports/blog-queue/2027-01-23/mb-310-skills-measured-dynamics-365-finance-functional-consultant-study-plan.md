MB-310 is the exam for the Microsoft Certified: Dynamics 365 Finance Functional Consultant Associate certification. This article summarizes the skills-measured outline in the Microsoft Learn study guide, as of August 14, 2026, plus the exam page facts and a study order. It covers the outline only and contains no exam questions.

## Format facts

| Item | From the Learn pages |
| --- | --- |
| Level | Intermediate |
| Time | 100 minutes |
| Delivery | Proctored, may include interactive components, scheduled through Pearson Vue |
| Languages | English and Japanese |
| Passing score | 700 or greater |
| Retake | After 24 hours following the first attempt, later waits vary |
| Renewal | Every 12 months by a free online assessment |
| Practice | Practice assessment on AI Skills Navigator, and an exam sandbox |

The study guide says localized versions follow the English update by about eight weeks. It adds that the bullets under each skill illustrate how it is assessed and that related topics may be covered, and that most questions cover generally available features, with possible questions on preview features if commonly used. Learn also advises registering for exams with a personal Microsoft account instead of a work or school account, because exam records tied to an organizational account are lost if you leave the organization.

## The candidate profile

You analyze business requirements and translate them into processes and solutions. You have a foundational understanding of accounting principles and financial operations as they relate to core tasks in finance and operations apps, manufacturing, retail, supply chain operations and service-based operations. The feature sets you specialize in are listed on the page, including general ledger, cash and bank, tax, receivable, credit and collections, subscription billing, payable, expense management, budgeting, fixed assets and asset leasing, cost accounting, consolidation and elimination, and agents.

## The five areas

| Area | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Implement financial management | 35-40% |
| Implement accounts receivable, credit, collections, and subscription billing | 15-20% |
| Implement and manage accounts payable and expenses | 10-15% |
| Manage budgeting | 10-15% |
| Manage fixed assets | 10-15% |

### Implement financial management (35-40%)

- **Chart of accounts and financial dimensions:** financial dimensions, main accounts and categories, ledger account aliases, balance control accounts, legal entity overrides, account structures and advanced rules, financial tags, derived dimensions and default templates.
- **Ledgers and currencies:** exchange types and rates, currency revaluation posting profile, accounting and reporting currencies, ledger and fiscal calendars, posting layers, ledger allocation rules.
- **Journals:** journal names and vouchers, journal controls and posting restriction rules, periodic journals, intercompany accounting, templates to edit journals in Excel, reversals, posting manually and with Excel, batch processing.
- **Cash and bank:** bank groups and accounts, payment formats, bank reconciliations including advanced, cash flow forecast setup, bank foreign currency revaluation, shared payment setup, netting.
- **Periodic processes:** closing and end-of-year processes, consolidation and elimination, financial period workspaces and closing schedules, Consolidation online, ledger settlements.
- **Taxes:** sales tax groups, item sales tax groups, tax codes, settlement periods, authorities and exemptions, tax ledger posting groups, settling and posting sales tax, withholding tax.

### Accounts receivable, credit, collections and subscription billing (15-20%)

- **Receivable:** customers and groups, shared customers, free text invoices including recurring, sales order invoices and credit memos with prepayments, payment methods, charges, posting profiles, customer payments, foreign currency revaluation, billing classification, intercompany invoices.
- **Credit and collections:** credit management, collections management and activities, delinquent customers, collection letters, aging definitions, credit holds, write-offs, interest notes.
- **Subscription billing:** sales documents from billing schedules, price updates and hold termination, contract billing setup, revenue and expense deferrals and recognition, recognition schedules, deferral defaults.

### Accounts payable and expenses (10-15%)

- **Payable:** invoice validation policies and matching, vendor groups and posting profiles, shared vendors, invoices and credit memos with or without purchase orders, prepayments, payment methods, charges, vendor bank accounts, foreign currency revaluation, vendor invoice journal, recurring invoices, payments and centralized payments, intercompany vendor invoices.
- **Expense management:** categories, per diem, mileage, intercompany and personal expenses, and expense reports with policies.

### Budgeting (10-15%)

- **Basic budgeting:** budget models, codes, allocation terms, transfer rules, budget register entries, comparing budget with actuals.
- **Budget controls:** parameters, fund availability options, control rules and groups, over-budget permissions, validating budget control on transactions.
- **Budget planning:** budget plans with scenarios and allocations, and the planning process including the organization hierarchy.

### Fixed assets (10-15%)

- **Implement and manage:** asset groups and attributes, posting profiles, books including derived books, depreciation profiles, creating assets, mapping groups and books.
- **Process transactions:** disposals through free text invoices, acquisitions through purchase orders, journals, inventory journals and projects, fixed asset budgets transferred to budgeting, depreciation including multi-company, split, reclassification, transfer and other transactions with the fixed asset journal.

## What changed on August 14, 2026

The change log says:

- Financial management has a decreased percentage. The other four areas have no percentage change.
- Implement cost management is removed.
- Major changes: ledgers and currencies, journals, periodic processes, accounts receivable, subscription billing, accounts payable and fixed asset transactions.
- Minor changes: chart of accounts and financial dimensions, taxes, credit and collections, expense management, budget controls, fixed assets, and the audience profile.
- Configure and manage budget planning is new.

If your material predates August 2026, compare it with this outline.

## A study order (my advice)

This is my suggestion, not a Microsoft plan:

1. Begin with dimensions, the chart of accounts and ledger setup. The largest area builds on them, and the other areas post into them.
2. Practice journals, including Excel templates and reversals, in a demo company, since journals appear in several areas.
3. Study receivable and payable as mirror images: posting profiles, payment methods, charges, prepayments, revaluation.
4. Spend time on the groups marked major, since their content changed the most.
5. Learn budgeting in the order the guide gives it: basic, controls, then planning, which is new.
6. Finish with the practice assessment and the exam sandbox to get used to the interface.
