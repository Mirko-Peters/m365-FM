Copilot in Outlook can summarize long threads, draft and coach emails, schedule meetings, prepare you for meetings and rank your incoming mail by priority. It works inside Outlook, with the mail and calendar data you can already reach. This article lists what Microsoft documents, where each feature appears and which limits matter.

## Summarize a thread

In new Outlook, a **Summary by Copilot** box appears at the top of a long email thread. In classic Outlook the control is **Summarize**. Microsoft Support says a summary condenses a conversation into key points and can include numbered citations that link to the source emails.

The FAQ adds that summaries try to match the email's language. Language detection for summaries is available on Android, iOS and Mac, with Windows and web listed as coming later. On Android, iOS and Mac you can set summaries to use the app's language instead, which resets when you close Outlook.

## Draft emails

Use the **Copilot** icon in the toolbar of a new mail window. In new Outlook the drop-down offers **Draft**. In classic Outlook it offers **Draft with Copilot**. You describe the message, then adjust it with controls such as **Make it shorter**, **Make it longer**, **Change Tone**, **Retry** and **Regenerate**, and keep the result with **Keep it**.

The FAQ says drafts can use thread context and organizational information, and that typing `/` starts a draft.

## Coaching

Copilot can suggest changes to tone, sentiment and clarity, and you can apply the suggestions to a draft at once. The welcome article only links to a separate coaching article, so use the FAQ's description as the summary.

## Prioritize the inbox and triage

The FAQ describes **Prioritize my inbox**. Copilot ranks incoming email as high, low or normal and gives a reason. Settings let you define topics to rank higher or lower. From Copilot chat you can also pin, flag, archive or delete one or many emails.

Limit: the FAQ says prioritization currently supports only Tier 1 languages, and it doesn't list them.

## Rules, scheduling and meeting prep

- **Rules:** Create and manage Outlook rules from plain-language requests.
- **Schedule with Copilot:** In new Outlook, a toolbar button builds a meeting invitation from a thread. The title and agenda are filled in, the thread is attached and the participants are added as attendees.
- **Scheduling by chat:** The FAQ says Copilot can schedule meetings with up to two other people or add events such as focus time.
- **Meeting prep:** Copilot gathers and summarizes meeting information, action items and related emails and chats. The FAQ warns the output can be generic when a meeting has little shared content or earlier communication.
- **Office days:** Suggests in-office days in a hybrid setup.
- **Copilot voice:** Lets you catch up on email and calendar by voice.

There is also a chat window beside Outlook that answers questions using organizational and web information.

## Where it works

| Item | What Microsoft Support says |
|---|---|
| Apps | Outlook for Microsoft 365 (Windows, Mac), new Outlook for Windows, Outlook for Android, Outlook for iOS |
| License | Microsoft Copilot (work) license. Chat needs a work or school account plus a Copilot subscription |
| Mailboxes | Primary, shared and delegate mailboxes you can access |
| Archive | In-Place or Online Archive results appear only when you search from the Outlook search bar with the Current Mailbox or All Folders scope |
| Not supported | Group mailboxes and mailboxes not hosted on Exchange Online |

## What it can't read

The FAQ says Copilot is not available on:

- signed or encrypted emails
- IRM-protected emails
- some MIP-labeled emails

It also can't access S/MIME or Double Key Encryption emails. If a summary button is missing on a thread, check whether the message is protected before you troubleshoot anything else.

## Using it well

Microsoft's own advice is to review, edit and verify Copilot output. The welcome article warns that output may be unexpected, that content drawn from the internet may contain inaccuracies, and that similar requests can return the same content. Treat drafts as a first pass.

A sensible order of use, based on the features above:

1. Start with summaries on long threads, since they link back to source emails you can check.
2. Use drafting for replies where you can state the point in a sentence.
3. Try prioritization only if your mail is in a supported language, and tune the topics.
4. Use coaching before sending messages with a tone risk.

## Common problems

- **Button missing:** Check the app. The welcome article lists specific apps and its Applies To list does not include Outlook on the web.
- **Nothing for a shared or group address:** Group mailboxes are not supported.
- **Prioritization ignores your language:** It supports only Tier 1 languages for now.
- **Encrypted thread:** Not supported.

The pages I read give no limits on email length or on how many messages a summary can cover, and no details on data retention. Ask your admin or check Microsoft's Copilot privacy documentation for those points.

## Which version of Outlook you use matters

Microsoft's instructions differ between classic Outlook, new Outlook and Mac, and button labels differ too. In classic Outlook you will see **Summarize** and **Draft with Copilot**. In new Outlook you will see **Summary by Copilot**, **Draft** and **Schedule with Copilot**. If a step in a tutorial doesn't match your screen, check which Outlook you have before assuming Copilot is missing. The welcome article gives step-by-step instructions for classic and new Outlook and Mac, but not for mobile.
