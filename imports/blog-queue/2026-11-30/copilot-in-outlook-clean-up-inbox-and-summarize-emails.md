Copilot in Outlook can summarize long email threads, help you triage with Copilot Chat and help you write inbox rules. Microsoft's documentation read for this article does not describe a single automatic cleanup button. Real cleanup comes from combining summaries, inbox rules and Outlook's own tools such as Sweep and Categories. Prioritize my inbox, which once flagged important mail, is being withdrawn.

**A note on sources.** The Microsoft Support articles for Copilot in Outlook could not be opened for this page. This article therefore relies on Microsoft Learn training pages, the Learn license overview, a Microsoft Q&A answer (marked AI-generated) and an independent report on the Prioritize my inbox withdrawal. Treat step details as a starting point and confirm menu names in your own Outlook.

## What Microsoft Learn says Copilot can do in Outlook

Two training modules describe the email use cases.

**Manage Email Efficiently With Microsoft Outlook And Copilot Chat** lists three objectives: organize your inbox to focus on high-priority messages, summarize email threads with Copilot Chat, and draft professional replies. Its units cover summarizing threads, drafting replies and creating inbox rules with Copilot Chat in Outlook.

**From inbox to impact** covers a broader set:

- Inbox triage with Copilot Chat to prioritize and sort mail
- Summaries of long email threads and attachments
- Draft Instructions for personalized messages
- Coaching by Copilot to refine drafts
- Converting an email thread into a meeting
- Writing Coach for more detailed feedback

The pages read describe no "clean up" feature by name. The training pages are landing pages, so exact prompts and click paths are not available there.

## Summarize an email thread

A Microsoft Q&A answer, which carries its own disclaimer that AI-generated content may be incorrect, gives these steps:

1. Open the long email thread in Outlook on desktop or web.
2. In the **Summary by Copilot** area at the top, select **Summarize**.
3. Read the summary. It may include numbered citations that link to specific emails.

On Outlook for Mac, the same answer says to select the conversation and use **Summarize** in the Summary by Copilot box. On Outlook for iOS and Android, tap the Copilot icon on the toolbar and the summary appears under the email. It also says the Mac feature requires the latest Outlook for Mac, not the legacy version.

If the Copilot button is missing, that answer suggests going to **File** > **Account**, selecting **Update License**, and restarting the Microsoft 365 apps. It also lists Draft with Copilot, Coaching by Copilot, Schedule with Copilot and Chat with Copilot as other entry points when licensed.

## Clean up the inbox in practice

Because no cleanup button is documented, use a combination.

### 1. Summarize before you decide

Long threads are the main source of inbox clutter. Summarize first, then reply, archive or delete. Copilot Chat training also lists summarizing attachments.

### 2. Create inbox rules with Copilot Chat

The Learn module has a unit named "Create inbox rules using Copilot Chat in Outlook" with an exercise. The idea is to describe the rule in plain language and let Copilot Chat set it up. Review the resulting rule before you rely on it, because the training page does not show the exact prompts.

### 3. Use Outlook's own organizing tools

The Learn module "Organize your inbox in new Outlook for Windows" names **Focused Inbox**, **Sweep**, **Categories** and **Copilot search** as intelligent features that reduce manual effort. It also mentions pinning messages and advanced search. Its units are Prioritize incoming mail, Explore other ways to organize your inbox and Find messages.

### 4. Triage with Copilot Chat

The "From inbox to impact" module describes using Copilot Chat in Outlook to prioritize and sort the inbox. Use it for a periodic review rather than expecting it to run on its own.

## What happens to Prioritize my inbox

Microsoft's message center item RM411302 describes Prioritize my inbox by Copilot as a feature that flags important emails, adds a short summary of why each matters, and lets you sort and filter by Copilot's priority. It lists general availability in April 2025 and platforms including web, desktop and mobile.

Office 365 for IT Pros reported in November 2026 that the feature is being withdrawn. Citing message center item MC1519861, updated October 22, 2026, it says withdrawal began October 26, 2026 and is due to finish by the end of March 2027. It adds that the feature disappears from Outlook menus automatically, that no user or administrator action is required and that Focused Inbox remains available. Microsoft's stated reason, as quoted in that report, is to simplify the Outlook Copilot experience and focus ongoing investments. That report is a secondary source. The message center item itself was not read, so check it in your admin center.

If your team relies on priority flags, plan to replace them with rules, categories and Focused Inbox.

## Licensing

The Learn license overview describes Copilot Chat in Outlook and Teams as an access point where Copilot Chat is aware of the content you have open. With Copilot Chat (Basic) and Microsoft 365 Copilot (Basic), Outlook content is available only through open content, and these licenses cannot use organizational data through Microsoft Graph when interacting with Copilot Chat. With Microsoft 365 Copilot (Premium), organizational data is used automatically through Microsoft Graph and Work IQ.

The Q&A answer lists several business subscriptions plus a Microsoft 365 Copilot license for Copilot in Outlook. Because that answer is AI-generated, confirm eligibility with your admin or Microsoft's current licensing pages.

## Common mistakes

- **Expecting an automatic cleanup.** None is documented in the pages read. Plan manual review plus rules.
- **Trusting a rule you did not test.** Check what a Copilot-created rule matches.
- **Relying on Prioritize my inbox.** It is being removed.
- **Using legacy Outlook for Mac.** The summary feature needs the latest Outlook for Mac, per the Q&A answer.
