When people say Microsoft 365 is used wrongly, the cause is often an adoption problem, not a product problem. The tools are rolled out, but nobody owns the change. Microsoft's own guidance treats adoption as a people program with stakeholders, champions, pilots and measurement. This article summarizes that guidance and shows how to read your own usage data.

## What Microsoft says about why it goes wrong

The Microsoft Learn change management strategy page, written for Microsoft Teams but referring to Microsoft 365 as a whole, says technology projects become challenged when little or no attention is paid to the human change management requirements. The page does not give a formal list of failure causes. Its recommendations point to the usual gaps:

- No sponsor or champions, so nobody speaks for the change.
- Too many scenarios at once.
- No single place for help, training and answers.
- No feedback channel.
- No success measures defined up front.
- Overwhelming users, which the page links to change fatigue.

## The eight best practices

Microsoft's list, in order:

1. Identify key stakeholders, champions and user profiles.
2. Identify and select business scenarios.
3. Run a pilot with business users, champions and IT professionals.
4. Design, launch and manage an adoption campaign with awareness materials, self-help and training in one location, a feedback mechanism and predefined success measures.
5. Build a champion program alongside the service deployment.
6. Provide a standard feedback method.
7. Measure and share success.
8. Adjust messaging and methods based on feedback, then repeat.

Microsoft says the steps can be adapted to organization size, but continued communication with stakeholders, champions, IT admins and users is key.

## Roles

- **Executive sponsors:** leaders whose participation is essential and who can communicate the value.
- **Service owners:** make sure people use the service and get value from it.
- **IT professionals:** use the usage reports in the Microsoft 365 admin center and sit on the project team.
- **Champions:** knowledgeable people who give peer coaching and translate the service into their team's reality.

Microsoft also advises engaging likely obstacles early, because they can become allies and often give valuable feedback, and investing time and attention in champions.

## Pick scenarios and pilot them

Segment users, for example mobile workers, people in constant meetings and those who struggle most with current tools. Segments most open to change are often good pilot targets. Select no more than three business scenarios for the first pilots. For a pilot, Microsoft's minimum steps are a regular stakeholder meeting, a feedback method, self-help documentation including videos and a kick-off meeting. Small organizations can use a public Teams channel for feedback. Larger ones can use a Viva Engage community.

An older Microsoft 365 blog post lists four success factors: stakeholders, scenarios, awareness and training. It was published in November 2017, so use it as background, not as current product guidance.

## Measure with Adoption Score

Adoption Score is a Microsoft 365 admin center report at **Reports** > **Adoption Score**. Facts from Microsoft Learn:

- It provides metrics, insights and recommended actions, calculated at the organization level, not for individuals.
- Categories include Communication, Meetings, Content collaboration, Teamwork, Mobility and AI Adoption, the last one only if Microsoft Copilot licenses are enabled. Each is worth 100 points.
- Scores reflect user actions from the last 28 days and update daily. The report offers 28-day and 180-day views.
- Technology experiences scores were removed in early 2026, so the score now equals the people experiences score and the maximum changed from 900 to 600.
- Peer benchmarks compare you with similar organizations by region, size, license types, industry and tenure.
- Many mature organizations operate well at 80 to 90 points per category, so prioritize what matches business goals.
- It requires a Microsoft 365 for business or Office 365 for enterprise subscription. A Viva Insights license is not required. It is not available in GCC High, GCC or DoD.

Organizational messages can nudge behaviors such as using cloud attachments in Outlook.

## A practical first month

1. Name an executive sponsor and a service owner.
2. Find champions in two or three departments.
3. Choose up to three scenarios and write the success measures.
4. Put help, training and a feedback channel in one place.
5. Run the pilot, then read Adoption Score and your usage reports.
6. Share one success story a month and repeat.

## What the pages do not say

The change management page is written for Teams and the other sources do not describe failure rates or typical user mistakes. Statements about why specific users "use it wrong" would be opinion, not documentation. The Adoption Score page does not state a minimum aggregation threshold and points to a separate privacy controls page.
