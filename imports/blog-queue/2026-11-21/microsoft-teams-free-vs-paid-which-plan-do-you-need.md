The short answer: Teams (free) suits small, informal use, with meetings of up to 60 minutes and 100 people. A paid plan is needed when meetings run longer, when more people join, or when you need recordings with transcripts, live captions, more storage or support. Microsoft's own pages give the limits below. Prices and numbers change, so confirm them on the pages linked at the end.

## Teams (free): what it includes

Microsoft's page for Teams (free) lists:

- Group meetings of up to 60 minutes and up to 100 participants. One-to-one meetings can run up to 30 hours.
- 5 GB of cloud storage per user.
- Unlimited chat, file sharing, tasks and polling, screen sharing, and encryption for meetings, chats, calls and files.
- Guests can join from a browser without an account and without installing an app.

Features that Microsoft lists only for Teams Essentials, such as live captions, Microsoft Whiteboard, recordings with transcripts and phone support, are not listed for the free plan.

## The paid business plans compared

Microsoft's business comparison page lists these plans for 1 to 300 users. The prices are US figures, per user per month, paid yearly, as shown when this article was written.

| Item | Teams Essentials | Microsoft 365 Business Basic | Microsoft 365 Business Standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | $4.00 | $7.00 | $23.50 (listed as Business Standard with Copilot) |
| Maximum meeting duration | 30 hours | 30 hours | 30 hours |
| Participant capacity | 300 | 300 | 300 |
| Cloud storage per user | 10 GB | 1 TB | 1 TB |
| Live captions | English only | Over 30 languages | Over 30 languages |
| Apps | Not listed | Web and mobile apps only | Desktop apps on up to 5 PCs or Macs, plus tablets and phones |

Essentials highlights chat, calling, video conferencing, file sharing, tasks, polling, meeting recordings with transcripts, and phone and web support. Basic and Standard list the Teams meeting and collaboration features as included in the version with Teams. Many Essentials feature cells on the comparison page were empty in the content I read, so some features could not be confirmed there.

One inconsistency is worth knowing. A Microsoft support article about moving from Teams Free (classic) says paid plans include 1 TB of file storage. The plan comparison lists 10 GB for Essentials. Treat the plan comparison as the plan-specific figure and verify in the admin center before you buy.

## How to decide

1. **Meeting length.** If regular meetings exceed 60 minutes, the free plan will end them. Paid plans list up to 30 hours.
2. **Group size.** More than 100 participants needs a paid plan. The business plans list up to 300.
3. **Storage and email.** If you need more than 5 GB per user, or a business mailbox, look at the Microsoft 365 Business plans. Essentials lists 10 GB, and the comparison lists no mailbox storage for it.
4. **Recordings and captions.** Recordings with transcripts and live captions are listed for the paid plans, not for the free plan.
5. **Support.** Phone and web support is listed for Essentials.
6. **Organization size.** The business plans are for up to 300 users. Larger organizations move to enterprise licensing.

## If you are on Teams Free (classic)

Microsoft retired Teams Free (classic) after April 12, 2023. The support article describes two paths:

- **Switch to the new Teams (free).** This gives a different feature set, and the data is not retained. Channels, chats and recurring meetings do not carry over.
- **Upgrade to a paid plan.** A full Microsoft 365 subscription or Microsoft Teams Essentials keeps teams, chats, files and permissions. Microsoft says there is no data loss during the upgrade.

The rules for the upgrade are strict. All users in the organization must be upgraded, and mixing paid and free users is not supported. You must buy a new Microsoft 365 subscription, because merging into an existing one is not supported. Organizations with more than 300 users need an enterprise license such as Office 365 E1. After the upgrade, member usernames must be updated and passwords reset in the admin portal.

To check your version, select your profile picture in the top-right corner of Teams. If Teams Free (classic) appears at the top of the menu, you are on the retired version.

## Licensing for larger organizations

Microsoft Learn explains that since April 1, 2024, new Enterprise customers who want Teams together with E1, E3 or E5 value buy two SKUs: the E1, E3 or E5 suite without Teams, and a Teams standalone license (Microsoft Teams Enterprise, or Microsoft Teams EEA). In the EEA and Switzerland this started October 1, 2023. Existing subscriptions that included Teams before April 1, 2024 can continue as before.

Microsoft 365 Business suites for up to 300 users, and the Frontline suites, remain available with and without Teams.

On top of a base license, Microsoft lists add-ons:

- **Microsoft Teams Premium** for personalized and intelligent meetings, enhanced meeting protection, and advanced management and reporting.
- **Audio Conferencing** so people can dial in by phone.
- **Teams Phone** and **Calling Plans** for calling.
- **Microsoft Teams Rooms**, where Teams Rooms Basic is free for up to 25 rooms.

## Common mistakes

- Planning a long webinar on the free plan and finding the meeting ends at 60 minutes.
- Upgrading only some users from Teams Free (classic). Microsoft says all users must be upgraded.
- Comparing storage without checking the plan. Essentials, Basic and Standard differ.
- Treating Teams Premium as a base plan. Microsoft lists it as an add-on license.
