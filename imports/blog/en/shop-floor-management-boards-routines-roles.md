Shop floor management is a routine for steering production at the place where the work happens. It uses visual boards, short daily meetings, a few clear measures and a fixed way to follow up problems, so the team and its managers react to deviations the same day. This article is for lean managers who want to set it up or repair it. It explains what the routine contains, what each role does and how to start with one cell. The routine works on small and large shop floors.

## What does shop floor management include?

The routine has four parts. They only work as a set.

- **A board** at the line or cell that shows the plan, the actual result and open problems.
- **A short daily meeting** at that board, held standing, at a fixed time.
- **Leader walks** to the gemba. Gemba is the Japanese word for "the real place", here the area where the work is done.
- **A follow-up loop** that turns every problem into an action with an owner and a due date.

You will also see the abbreviation SFM, and the terms "daily management" or "tiered management". They describe the same idea: manage by looking at the process, not by reading reports a week later.

The goal is speed of reaction. A weekly report tells you about a late order after it is late. A board at the cell tells you at 9 a.m. that the first-hour output is behind, while there is still time to move a person or a fixture. It also gives the team a voice. Operators see the plan, see the gap and can raise a problem the same hour.

Shop floor management is not a reporting tool. It is a decision routine. If nobody decides anything at the board, the board is decoration.

## What belongs on a shop floor board?

A good shop floor board answers three questions at a glance: What was the plan? What did we do? What is in our way?

The [Lean Enterprise Institute describes one plant](https://www.lean.org/the-lean-post/articles/build-your-house-of-production-on-a-stable-foundation/) where every cell board shows the same information in the same place: daily schedule attainment in the centre, a two-week trend along the bottom, first-time quality by machine and cell, hourly counts, and new issues at the upper right. The point is not the exact layout. The point is that it never changes from cell to cell, so a manager can read any board in seconds.

A practical starting set for one cell:

1. **Safety:** open hazards and incidents.
2. **Quality:** first-time-quality or scrap, plan versus actual.
3. **Delivery:** units planned and made, per hour or per shift.
4. **Cost or productivity:** one measure the team can influence, such as hours per unit or minutes of downtime.
5. **Open issues:** a short list with owner, due date and status.

Keep each measure simple enough that an operator can update it by hand. Use green and red for "on plan" and "off plan", and write the reason for every red. A red with no reason teaches nothing.

Five or six items are enough. Typical KPIs for a cell are output, first-time quality, productivity and downtime. If you add every available KPI, the one that matters gets lost. Pick the measures that this team can change within a shift. Plant-level figures such as annual cost per unit belong higher up.

## How does the daily meeting run?

The daily meeting is the engine of the routine. Its form is simple, and the discipline is in keeping it that way.

1. **Hold it at the board, standing, at the same time every day.** Ten minutes is normal for a cell, up to 15 for a department.
2. **Review yesterday.** Read the plan-versus-actual numbers aloud. Name the biggest gap and its cause in one sentence.
3. **Review today.** Check the plan for the shift: people, materials, machines and known risks, such as a bottleneck machine waiting for repair.
4. **Add new problems.** Write each one on the issue list. Give it an owner and a due date.
5. **Check old actions.** Ask whether the open actions are done. Close them or reset the date.
6. **End on time.** Anything that needs analysis happens after the meeting, with the owner and the people involved.

Do not solve problems in the meeting. When ten people stand around one machine problem for 20 minutes, nine of them lose time. Assign the problem and move on.

## Who does what: the roles

Clear roles keep the routine alive when people are busy.

- **Operators** keep their data current, say what is blocking them and suggest fixes. They own the facts of the shift.
- **Team leaders** run the daily meeting and update the board. They own the first reaction to a deviation.
- **Supervisors and department heads** attend the meetings of their cells, check that actions are closed and escalate what the cell cannot solve.
- **The plant manager** walks the floor on a fixed routine, asks questions at the boards and removes obstacles that sit above the cell.

Leader behaviour matters most. The Lean Enterprise Institute describes [leader standard work](https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/leader-standard-work/) as shifting managers from being the primary problem solvers to building the problem-solving capability of employees. It lists five day-to-day tools: gemba walks, reflection meetings, responding to andons, creating accountability and mentoring people. An andon is a signal that shows an abnormal condition in an area.

In the plant described in the LEI article, managers walk the floor twice a day at fixed times and review the cell boards with their direct reports. The plant manager's daily question is whether the plan was met and, if not, what will be done about it. A fixed time and a fixed question make the walk a habit. A good [gemba walk](https://www.m365.fm/entity/gemba-walk/) is therefore part of shop floor management, not a substitute for it.

## Example: one board, one shift

This is an illustration with made-up numbers, not a measured result.

A cell builds a family of valve housings. The plan for the first shift is 240 units, 30 per hour over eight hours.

- At 10 a.m. the board shows 90 units made against 90 planned. Green.
- At noon it shows 150 against 150 planned. Green.
- At 2 p.m. it shows 190 against 210 planned. Red. The team leader writes the reason: "tool change took 35 minutes, wrong insert delivered".
- At the end of the shift the count is 204 against 240. Attainment is 204 / 240 = 85%.

At the next morning meeting, the team reads the 85% and the reason for the biggest gap. The action is: "Kit inserts for the next three jobs at the cell before the shift. Owner: material handler. Due: Friday." On Friday, the team checks the action. If the next week shows no tool-change problems, the action is closed. This is the whole loop: plan, actual, reason, action, check. It runs in minutes, and each loop removes one cause of lost output. Over weeks, this is how a team practises [continuous improvement](https://www.m365.fm/entity/continuous-improvement/) every day.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting with too many boards.** Ten half-used boards teach the team that the routine is optional. Start with one cell and make it work.
- **Showing KPIs that nobody can influence.** A board full of plant-level figures gives the team no reason to look at it.
- **Updating the board for visitors.** If the data is changed before an audit, trust is gone. Update it live, by hand if needed, and show the bad days.
- **Using the meeting to solve problems.** It turns a 10-minute meeting into 40 minutes, and people stop coming.
- **No follow-up.** A problem without an owner and a due date stays on the list for months. Review old actions at every meeting.
- **Leaders who skip the walk.** If managers do not come to the board, the team concludes that the board does not matter.

## How software can help

Paper boards work well at the start. Later the plan changes during the day, and a handwritten board lags behind it. Software can take today's plan from ERP data, show it as time-based tasks and collect actual progress at the cell in real time, so the dashboard shows today's numbers, not yesterday's. iFAKT's [Polaris Operator](https://www.m365.fm/entity/polaris-operator/) is one example. It brings production plans to the shop floor as time-based tasks and feeds progress back to planning in real time. Use software to remove double entry. Keep the meeting at the board.

## Next step

Choose one cell this week, set up a simple board with five measures and hold a 10-minute shop floor management meeting at the same time every day for four weeks. Review the board with the team at the end of that period and decide what to keep, what to drop and which cell comes next.
