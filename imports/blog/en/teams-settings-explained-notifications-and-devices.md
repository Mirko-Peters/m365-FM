Teams has a lot of settings, and they are spread over several pages. This guide shows where the most useful ones are and what each does. It covers notifications and devices, because those are the pages Microsoft documents in detail and the ones people change most. Menu names can differ slightly between the new Teams, classic Teams, the web app and mobile, and some options exist only on Windows. Admins can also restrict what you can change.

## How to open Settings

1. Sign in to Teams on your desktop.
2. Select **Settings and more** (the three dots) next to your profile picture.
3. Select **Settings**.

At the top there is a search box. Use it to find an option by category, title or keyword when you don't know which page it is on.

## Notifications and activity

Notification settings are under **Settings** > **Notifications and activity**. Microsoft Support lists these pages, in this order.

**General.** Here you find **Mute all notifications (except for calls and meetings)**, which Microsoft lists for Windows only. You can also choose whether Teams plays sounds with notifications, with urgent and priority contact notifications and with incoming calls, requests to join and meeting starts. Two switches matter for focus work: whether message and content previews show in notifications, and whether notifications show during calls and meetings.

**Sound.** Pick a sound for each notification type. Custom sounds are available only in Teams for Windows and on the web.

**Display.** Choose how much detail previews show, where on the screen banners appear (bottom right, top right, bottom left or top left) and the preview size (Comfy or Compact).

**Missed activity emails.** Choose how often Teams sends a summary email, or turn the emails off.

**Chats and channels.** For each type of notification, such as mentions, replies or reactions, pick where it appears or select **Off**.

**Meetings.** This page controls meeting start notifications, the meeting join bar and **Meet now** start notifications. It also controls meeting chat notifications. Microsoft says notifications for accepted meetings are on by default, while chats of tentative or no-response meetings are muted until you join or send a message.

**Presence.** Select **Manage notifications** to add or remove contacts whose status changes you want to be told about.

**Calendar.** Choose where each calendar notification appears, or turn it off.

**Apps.** This page shows up after you get your first notification from an app. Each app has a toggle, and some offer **Activity**, **Activity and banner** or off.

### The two places a notification can go

For most types, Microsoft describes two places: **Activity and banner** (a banner plus an entry in the feed) and **Activity** only (just the feed). You can also select **Off**. A good starting setup for many people is banners for direct mentions and chats, and feed only for channel traffic.

### Channel and thread notifications

Channel alerts are not on the main settings page. Hover over a channel, select **More options** > **Channel notifications** and switch new-message notifications on or off. You can choose **Activity and banner** or **Activity** only. In the Threads layout you can also include thread replies and follow all new threads.

Teams follows threads automatically when you reply to them, are @mentioned in them or start them from your message. To follow one yourself, select **Follow** or **More options** > **Follow thread**. Followed threads appear at the top of your chat and channels list.

The **Unread**, **@Mentions** and **Followed threads** quick views change which conversations are listed. They do not change which notifications you receive.

### What the notification page does not cover

Microsoft's article does not describe quiet hours, and it points to separate pages for mobile problems and for muting notifications during a meeting. If notifications behave differently on your phone, use the mobile troubleshooting page that Microsoft links from the notification article.

## Devices

Open **Settings** > **Devices**. Microsoft splits it into audio and video.

**Audio options**

- **Manage devices** connects Bluetooth-enabled Teams hardware such as displays or desk phones.
- **Audio settings** let you pick your primary audio device, speaker and microphone.
- **Mic sensitivity** adjusts the microphone automatically.
- **Device buttons** syncs the buttons on your device. The Teams button always works.
- **Test call** checks call quality.
- **Noise suppression** reduces background noise.
- **Voice isolation** keeps only your voice audible. It requires a voice profile first.
- **High fidelity music mode** improves sound when you share music in meetings or calls.
- **Spatial audio** makes meetings sound more immersive.
- **Secondary ringer** rings another device for incoming calls. The default is **None**.

**Video options**

- **Automatic camera controls** adjusts the camera automatically, or opens your camera settings.
- **Camera** selects the camera.
- **Adjust brightness** improves video in low light.
- **Soft focus** smooths your appearance.
- **Green screen** improves the look of virtual backgrounds.
- **Preview** shows how you look.

Run a **Test call** before an important meeting, and check **Preview** if your picture looks dark.

## A quick tidy-up that works for most people

1. Under **General**, decide whether you need previews on while you present. Turn them off if you share your screen often.
2. Under **Chats and channels**, set noisy types to **Activity** only.
3. Under **Missed activity emails**, lower the frequency or turn the emails off if you already check Teams daily.
4. In each busy channel, use **Channel notifications** to reduce alerts.
5. Under **Devices**, choose your headset and camera explicitly, so Teams does not pick a laptop microphone.

## What else is in Settings

The settings sidebar also holds other areas such as status, profile and accessibility. Microsoft documents these on separate pages, for example for changing your profile picture, setting a status message and screen reader support. The pages I read for this article do not describe General and Privacy options in detail, so I don't list them here. Check Microsoft's own pages or your admin's guidance for those.
