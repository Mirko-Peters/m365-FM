Teams has a lot of settings, and they are spread over several pages. This guide shows where the most useful ones are and what each does. It covers notifications and devices, because those are the pages Microsoft documents in detail and the ones people change most. Menu names can differ slightly between the Teams desktop app, the web app and mobile, and some options exist only on Windows.

## How to open Settings

1. Sign in to Teams on your desktop.
2. Select **Settings and more** (the three dots) next to your profile picture.
3. Select **Settings**.

Microsoft Support lists five pages in Settings: **General**, **Chat and channels**, **Notifications and activity**, **Privacy** and **Devices**. The notification and device articles also mention a search box in Settings. Use it to find an option by keyword when you don't know which page it is on.

Be careful with the name **General**. Settings has its own **General** page for things like startup behavior and language. The notification pages below also contain a **General** page, which you reach through **Notifications and activity**. They are different pages.

## Notifications and activity

Notification settings are under **Settings** > **Notifications and activity**. Microsoft Support covers these parts, in this order. Sounds, display and missed activity emails sit inside its general notifications section.

**General.** Here you find **Mute all notifications (except for calls and meetings)**, which Microsoft lists for Windows only. You can also choose whether Teams plays sounds with notifications, with urgent and priority contact notifications and with incoming calls, requests to join and meeting starts. Two switches matter for focus work: whether message previews show in notifications, and whether notifications show during calls and meetings. There is also a **Test notification** button.

**Sound.** Pick a sound for each notification type. Custom sounds are available only in Teams for Windows and on the web.

**Display.** Choose which corner of the screen notifications appear in and the preview size (Comfy or Compact).

**Missed activity emails.** Choose how often Teams sends a summary email, or turn the emails off.

**Chats and channels.** Next to each notification type, select the dropdown menu to choose where you want the notifications to appear, or select **Off**.

**Meetings.** This page controls meeting start notifications, the meeting join bar and **Meet now** start notifications. It also controls meeting chat notifications. Microsoft says notifications for accepted meetings are on by default, while chats of tentative or no-response meetings are muted until you join or send a message.

**Presence.** Select **Manage notifications** to add contacts whose status changes you want to be told about, and select **Turn off** to stop tracking someone.

**Calendar.** For each type, such as invites, updates, cancellations and forwarded meetings, choose where it appears or turn it off.

**Apps.** This page shows up after you get your first notification from an app. Each app has a toggle, and some offer **Activity**, **Activity and banner** or off.

### The two places a notification can go

For channels, Microsoft names two options: **Activity and banner** and **Activity** only. It describes the Activity feed as a timeline of notifications and updates but does not define a banner any further. Chat and calendar notifications also have an **Off** option. As a suggestion, many people like banners for direct mentions and chats, and feed only for channel traffic.

### Channel and thread notifications

Channel alerts are not on the main settings page. Hover over a channel, select **More options** > **Channel notifications** and choose **Activity and banner** or **Activity** only. Microsoft says you can turn on or off notifications for all new messages or posts, depending on your layout. In the Threads layout you can also select **Include thread replies** to be notified when others reply to a post. Selecting **Follow all new threads** adds new replies to **Followed threads**, but Microsoft says this won't notify you in Activity.

Teams follows threads automatically when you reply to them, are @mentioned in them or someone starts a thread from your message. To follow one yourself, select **Follow** or **More options** > **Follow thread**. Followed threads appear at the top of your chat and channels list.

The **Unread**, **@Mentions** and **Followed threads** quick views change which conversations are listed. They do not change which notifications you receive.

### Related Microsoft pages

Microsoft's notification article links to a page on muting notifications during a meeting and one on customizing channel notifications. Use those if you want quiet during one meeting or finer control per channel.

## Devices

Open **Settings** > **Devices**. Microsoft splits it into audio and video.

**Audio options**

- **Manage devices** connects Bluetooth-enabled Teams devices such as displays or desk phones.
- **Audio settings** let you pick your primary audio device, speaker and microphone.
- **Automatically adjust mic sensitivity** adjusts the microphone so you are consistently heard.
- **Sync device buttons** lets you use all the buttons on your device. The Teams button always works.
- **Make a test call** checks your call quality.
- **Noise suppression** reduces background noise.
- **Voice isolation** isolates your voice from background noise and other voices. You must set up a voice profile first.
- **High fidelity music mode** gives richer sound when you share music in a meeting or call.
- **Spatial audio** makes meetings sound more immersive.
- **Secondary ringer** rings another device for all incoming calls. The default is **None**.

**Video options** (under **Change video settings**)

- **Automatically adjust camera controls** adjusts the camera automatically, or opens your camera settings.
- **Camera** selects your preferred camera.
- **Adjust brightness** improves video in low light.
- **Soft focus** smooths your appearance.
- **Green screen** improves your look when you use a virtual background.
- A preview shows how your video looks.

Run **Make a test call** before an important meeting, and check the preview if your picture looks dark.

## A quick tidy-up that works for most people

1. Under the general notification options, decide whether you need previews on while you present. Turn them off if you share your screen often.
2. Under **Chats and channels**, turn off or reduce the notification types you don't need.
3. In the **Missed activity emails** options, lower the frequency or turn the emails off if you already check Teams daily.
4. In each busy channel, use **Channel notifications** to reduce alerts.
5. Under **Devices**, choose your headset and camera yourself, so you know which devices Teams uses.

## What else is in Settings

Microsoft's page on opening Teams settings describes the other pages briefly:

- **General:** startup behavior, suggested replies, language, spell check and out-of-office settings.
- **Chat and channels:** a combined or separate view for chats and channels.
- **Privacy:** Do not disturb access, blocked contacts, read receipts and attendance reports.

I don't describe those pages step by step here. Check Microsoft's own page for the details.
